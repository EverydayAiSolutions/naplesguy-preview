What kids learn in each grade:
a Florida parent’s guide

Plain-English summaries of what students work toward in Pre-K through 12th grade in Florida, with official lessons, practice, videos, and testing information for families in Naples and Collier County.

Jeff Silberman, The Naples Guy, put this guide together so parents can see, in everyday language, what Florida schools work toward each year. The grade pages are based on the Florida Department of Education’s B.E.S.T. parent guides, Florida’s Early Learning and Developmental Standards, Florida’s State Academic Standards, and official assessment pages, all checked in November 2026. A child’s classroom may move a little faster or slower, and some students follow a different path, so families should use this as a starting point and confirm details with the teacher and the official links.

What the summaries are

Each grade page restates, in plain English, skills from official Florida parent guides and academic standards. It is not a complete list of every benchmark, and it is not a report card for any child.

FAST, EOC, and science

Testing in Florida schools

Florida’s statewide assessments are described by the Florida Department of Education. Windows below are from the official 2026–2027 statewide schedule. A school chooses actual testing days inside those windows, so families should confirm dates with Collier County Public Schools.

FAST progress monitoring

The Florida Assessment of Student Thinking (FAST) includes ELA Reading for VPK through grade 10 and Mathematics for VPK through grade 8. It is given three times a year: PM1 near the start of the year, PM2 in the middle, and PM3 at the end. PM1 and PM2 are for information. PM3 is the end-of-year score used for school accountability in the grades where that applies.

Official 2026–2027 windows: PM2 is November 30, 2026, through January 22, 2027, for grades K–10 ELA Reading and K–8 Mathematics. PM3 is April 12–May 28, 2027, for grades K–2, and May 3–28, 2027, for grades 3–10 ELA Reading and 3–8 Mathematics.

Source: Florida Department of Education, FAST Assessments. Source: 2026–2027 Florida Statewide Assessment Program Schedule.

FAST assessments 2026–27 statewide schedule (PDF)

Writing, science, and end-of-course tests

B.E.S.T. Writing is given in grades 4–10. Statewide Science assessments are given in grades 5 and 8. Students enrolled in Algebra 1, Geometry, Biology 1, Civics, or U.S. History take the matching end-of-course assessment, which counts as 30 percent of the final course grade.

Students must pass Grade 10 FAST ELA Reading (or a concordant score) and the Algebra 1 end-of-course assessment (or a comparative score) to meet standard diploma assessment requirements. Official pages explain current rules, including options for students with disabilities.

Source: Florida Department of Education, K-12 Student Assessment. Source: Academic Advisement, students entering grade 9 in 2023–2024 and thereafter.

CPALMS is the State of Florida’s official source for standards, course descriptions, and a large library of reviewed lessons, tutorials, and practice resources.

Khan Academy, PBS, and Reading Rockets

Khan Academy offers practice exercises and instructional videos in math and other subjects. It is a helpful supplement; Florida classrooms follow the B.E.S.T. Standards, which may not match every Khan Academy lesson in order.

Questions

Questions parents often ask

These answers come from the official pages linked in this guide. Details can change, so families should confirm them there.

What standards do Florida public schools use?

Florida public schools use the Benchmarks for Excellent Student Thinking (B.E.S.T.) Standards for English language arts and mathematics. Science and social studies follow Florida’s State Academic Standards, which families can look up by grade on CPALMS, the state’s official standards site. Voluntary Prekindergarten uses the Florida Early Learning and Developmental Standards for children ages 4 to kindergarten. Source: Florida Department of Education, Standards & Instructional Support. Source: Florida Early Learning and Developmental Standards, 4 Years Old to Kindergarten.

What is FAST, and how often is it given?

The Florida Assessment of Student Thinking (FAST) is Florida’s coordinated screening and progress-monitoring program. It includes ELA Reading for VPK through grade 10 and Mathematics for VPK through grade 8, given three times a year (PM1, PM2, and PM3). PM1 and PM2 are for information. PM3 is the end-of-year score used for school accountability in the grades where that applies. Source: Florida Department of Education, FAST Assessments.

Does every child in Collier County take the same courses?

No. This guide describes typical grade-level expectations in Florida. A student may take advanced or honors courses, a different math sequence, Exceptional Student Education services, or English language learner supports. Families should confirm the child’s actual courses with the school. Source: Collier County Public Schools academics pages.

Where can families find official worksheets and lessons?

CPALMS has reviewed lessons and resources aligned to Florida standards. FloridaStudents.org has tutorials created by Florida teachers. The Florida Department of Education parent guides include home activities. Khan Academy, PBS LearningMedia, and Reading Rockets also offer practice and family activities that can supplement schoolwork. Source: CPALMS. Source: Florida Department of Education parent guides.

Where can Naples families find books and homework space?

Collier County Public Library has 10 locations, including the Headquarters Library at 2385 Orange Blossom Dr, Naples. Youth cards are available for children from birth through age 17, and the official site lists storytimes and other events. A librarian can also help a family find books and digital resources. Source: Collier County Public Library.
