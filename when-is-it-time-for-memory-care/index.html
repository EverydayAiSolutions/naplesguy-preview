Alzheimer's and dementia care in Naples

When Is It Time for Memory Care?

When is it time for memory care? For most Naples families, the answer comes when safety at home can no longer be kept with the help available: wandering, missed medications, unsafe cooking or driving, nights without sleep, or a caregiver at the breaking point.

The signs

Signs it may be time for memory care

Safety

  • Wandering, getting lost, or leaving home at night.
  • Leaving the stove or water on.
  • Missed or doubled medications.
  • Falls, or unsafe driving.

Health and behavior

  • Weight loss or skipped meals.
  • Sundowning that keeps the house awake.
  • Fear or agitation the family cannot calm.
  • Refusing help with bathing or hygiene.

The caregiver

  • No full night of sleep in weeks.
  • Missed work or missed doctor visits of their own.
  • Feeling alone, angry or exhausted.
  • A doctor saying the current plan is not safe.

Early signs of Alzheimer's are explained by the Alzheimer's Association: 10 Warning Signs. Only a doctor can diagnose dementia.

Florida rules

What Florida law says about memory care

Today, a Florida assisted living facility that advertises special care for Alzheimer's or related disorders must have an awake staff member on duty at all hours if it has 17 or more residents, offer activities designed for people with cognitive impairment, provide a safe physical environment, and use staff trained under section 430.5025 (section 429.178).

A 2026 law creates a separate memory care services license (section 429.076). AHCA must adopt the minimum standards by June 1, 2027, and the rules will name the advertising terms, such as memory care and dementia care, that require the license. Facilities licensed before the rules take effect have six months after that date to obtain it.

Any assisted living facility must notify a physician within 30 days after staff notice signs of dementia or cognitive impairment, and cannot keep a resident who needs 24-hour nursing supervision, with an exception for residents in hospice (section 429.26).

Good to know

Questions people ask

What are the signs it is time for memory care?

Common signs include wandering or getting lost, leaving the stove on, missed or doubled medications, falls, weight loss, sundowning that keeps the household awake, agitation the family cannot calm, and a caregiver whose own health is slipping. One serious safety event is often enough.

What is the difference between assisted living and memory care?

Memory care is assisted living designed for people with Alzheimer's or another dementia, with secured design, trained staff and activities for people with cognitive changes. Under Florida law, a facility that advertises special care for Alzheimer's must, among other things, offer activities designed for people with cognitive impairment and use staff trained under section 430.5025.

Is Florida changing how memory care is licensed?

Yes. A 2026 law (section 429.076, Florida Statutes) creates a memory care services license for assisted living facilities. AHCA must adopt the minimum standards by June 1, 2027, and those rules will list terms, such as memory care and dementia care, that a facility cannot advertise without the license.

Can someone with dementia stay in regular assisted living?

Sometimes, in the early stages. Florida law requires a facility to notify a physician within 30 days when a resident shows signs of dementia or cognitive impairment, and a facility cannot keep a resident who needs 24-hour nursing supervision.

Who can help a Naples family decide?

The Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline (800-272-3900) offers care consultations, the Alzheimer's Support Network in Naples (239-262-8388) runs weekly support groups, and Jeff Silberman (609-575-8626) helps families tour and compare memory care communities.
