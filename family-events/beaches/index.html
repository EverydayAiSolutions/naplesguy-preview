Naples Beaches Guide:
parking, restrooms & access

A practical guide to public beaches and beach accesses in Naples and nearby Bonita Springs. Each card covers parking, restrooms, showers, lifeguards, accessibility, dogs, food, playgrounds, and hours when the official park or city page lists them. If a detail is not published there, this page says “Check official page” rather than guessing. Hours, fees, and construction can change, so confirm on the official link before you go.

How beach parking works

Collier County beach parks

County residents can get a free beach parking permit with proof of residency at county park community centers, some libraries, and Tax Collector offices. The permit must be permanently adhered to the lower left corner of the driver’s-side windshield and stays with that vehicle. Visitors who are not eligible for the permit pay a flat $10 per day through Mobile Pay to Park (text the code or scan the QR code on the lot sign; no app required).

Collier County beach parking information · Mobile Pay to Park sheet (PDF)

City of Naples beach ends

Every city beach parking space requires a payment or a beach parking permit. City residents and City property owners can get a free permit at City Hall, 735 8th Street South. Collier County residents and county property owners can get a free permit at the county locations above. Visitors pay at meters or pay stations: $5.00 per hour, $2.50 minimum. Twenty-four street ends are permit-only. No parking from 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. A Florida disability parking permit holder may park in any space without payment under Florida Statute 316.1964, with the limits on the City page.

Florida State Parks nearby

Delnor-Wiggins Pass State Park and Lovers Key State Park charge their own entrance fees. A Collier or City beach parking permit does not replace those fees on the official pages reviewed. Current published rates: Delnor-Wiggins is $6 per vehicle (limit eight people), $4 for a single-occupant vehicle; Lovers Key is $8 per vehicle (2 to 8 people), $4 for a single-occupant vehicle or motorcycle.

Location
11135 Gulfshore Drive, Naples, FL 34108

Parking
Admission is paid at remote kiosks (credit or debit). Put the printed receipt on the dashboard. Fees: $6.00 per vehicle (limit eight people); $4.00 for a single-occupant vehicle; $2.00 for pedestrians, bicyclists, extra passengers, or passengers in a vehicle with a holder of an Annual Individual Entrance Pass. Boat-ramp use is $5.00 in addition to admission. Vehicles over 24 feet, including buses, motor homes, and vehicles with trailers, must park at the boat launch. When lots are at capacity, the park may temporarily close to new vehicles; pedestrians and bicyclists may still enter, and cars may not wait at the entrance. Collier County's Paradise Beach Trolley is described as a shuttle from the Conner Park parking lot on Bluebill Avenue to the park entrance. This is a Florida State Park fee, not Collier County beach parking.
Parking spaces
Four parking lots along the Gulf for beach access, plus vessel-trailer parking at the boat launch. An official space count is not published on the pages reviewed.
Restrooms
Portable restrooms are at each parking area. Hand sanitizer is provided. Running water is not available, and drinking water is not available.
Showers
Running water is not available, according to the official amenities page. Check official page for any temporary rinse stations.
Lifeguards
Lifeguard hours are not listed on the official park page. Check official page.
Accessibility
Beach wheelchairs are free, first come, first served, in parking areas one and three. An electric beach wheelchair is also free, first come, first served; it is available at 8 a.m. and must be returned to the shop by closing. Ask at the office or call 239-409-0637 to check availability.
Dogs
Pets are allowed in paved areas of the park, on a 6-foot handheld leash, but they are not permitted on the beach. Service animals are welcome in all areas of the park, as required by law.
Food and concessions
A food concession is not listed on the official pages reviewed. Check official page.
Playground
Not listed on the official park pages reviewed.
Shelling and wildlife
The park is described as a relatively undisturbed barrier-island beach. Check official pages for rules on wildlife and collecting.
Best for
A quieter, more natural state-park beach. Temporary restrooms are in use, and there is no drinking water, so plan ahead.
Hours
8 a.m. to sunset, 365 days a year.

Effective Sept. 28, 2026, entrance fees are paid at remote off-grid kiosks in the park and at the boat ramp.

Effective April 18, 2025, the park asks visitors to follow posted signs and avoid closed areas.

Questions families ask

Beach parking, dogs, lifeguards, and the pier

How does beach parking work for Collier County residents and visitors?

Collier County residents can get a free beach parking permit with proof of residency at county park community centers, some libraries, and Tax Collector offices. The permit must be permanently stuck to the lower left corner of the driver's-side windshield and stays with that vehicle. Visitors who are not eligible for that permit pay a flat $10 per day through Mobile Pay to Park at the county beach parks listed on the official sheet. Hours, lot rules, and locations can change, so check the official parking page before you go.

Source: Collier County Parks beach parking information. Source: Mobile Pay to Park information sheet.

How does City of Naples beach parking work?

Every City of Naples beach parking space requires a payment or a beach parking permit. City residents and City property owners can get a free permit at City Hall. Collier County residents and county property owners can get a free permit at county locations. Visitors pay at meters or pay stations, currently $5.00 per hour with a $2.50 minimum. Twenty-four street ends are permit-only; visitors without a permit should use the metered visitor accesses instead. Parking is prohibited from 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. A Florida disability parking permit holder may park in any space without payment under Florida Statute 316.1964, with the limits on the City page.

Source: City of Naples beach parking information. Source: City beach parking FAQ.

Is there free beach parking in Naples?

For visitors without a resident permit, the official pages reviewed do not list a free public beach parking lot in the City of Naples or at Collier County beach parks. City pages say beach parking is not free. County beach parks charge visitors $10 per day. Florida State Parks at Delnor-Wiggins Pass and Lovers Key charge an entrance fee. People with a City or Collier resident beach parking permit can park free at City and county beach lots that accept the permit. State park fees are separate and are not waived by those permits on the pages reviewed.

Source: City beach parking FAQ. Source: Collier County beach parking. Source: Delnor-Wiggins hours and fees. Source: Lovers Key hours and fees.

Are dogs allowed on Naples and Collier County beaches?

Collier County beach park pages reviewed say no dogs are permitted. The City of Naples beach parking rules say no animals are allowed on the beach. At Delnor-Wiggins Pass and Lovers Key state parks, pets may be in paved areas on a 6-foot leash but are not allowed on the beach; service animals are welcome in all park areas as required by law. On Keewaydin Island public lands, Rookery Bay rules prohibit unleashed domestic animals, except animals assisting a person with a disability. Always check posted signs.

Source: individual Collier County beach park pages linked on this guide. Source: City of Naples beach parking information. Source: Delnor-Wiggins amenities. Source: Lovers Key amenities. Source: Rookery Bay Reserve.

Which beaches have lifeguards?

North Collier Fire Control and Rescue District provides lifeguard services at Vanderbilt Beach Public Access as of July 1, 2026, with a tower at the Vanderbilt Beach turnaround and chairs at beach markers 28A and 29. Lifeguard coverage is not listed on the other official park pages reviewed for this guide. That does not, by itself, prove a beach has no lifeguards on a given day. Check the official park or fire-district page before you go.

Source: North Collier Fire Ocean Rescue.

Which beaches have restrooms, and which are easier to reach with a wheelchair?

Official pages list restrooms at Vanderbilt Beach Park, Bluebill Beach Access, and Clam Pass Park, plus portable restrooms at each Delnor-Wiggins parking area and restrooms at Lovers Key. Lowdermilk Park has restrooms and showers. During the Naples Pier rebuild, pier restrooms are closed, and the City says restrooms are available at the Broad Avenue South and 13th Avenue South bypasses.

Beach wheelchairs or mats are listed at Lowdermilk Park (mat, plus wheelchairs for rent at the concession), Clam Pass Park (wheelchair rentals), Delnor-Wiggins Pass (wheelchairs in parking areas one and three, plus an electric wheelchair), and Lovers Key (free beach and trail wheelchairs). 4th Avenue South is marked with a handicap ramp. Check official pages, because equipment is often first come, first served.

Source: the official park pages linked on each beach card in this guide.

Is the Naples Pier open, and how do I get to the beach there?

The historic pier is closed for the Naples Pier Rebuild Project and is scheduled to reopen in July 2027. The pier itself, its stairs, restrooms, showers, and 12th Avenue South west of Gulf Shore Boulevard South are closed. The western one-third of the pier parking lot remains open. The City is keeping beach access open with pedestrian bypasses at Broad Avenue South and 13th Avenue South, and says restrooms are available at both.

Source: Naples Pier. Source: Naples Pier rebuild project.

How do I get to Keewaydin Island?

Keewaydin Island, also called Key Island, has no bridge or public road. Rookery Bay describes it as an unbridged barrier island that people visit by boat. There is no public parking lot on the island. Follow Rookery Bay rules, including no glass, no removing natural resources, and staying out of posted nesting areas, especially from March through August. This page does not name a private ferry or taxi.

Source: Explore the Rookery Bay Reserve.
