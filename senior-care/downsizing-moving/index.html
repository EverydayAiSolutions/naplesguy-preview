02Deciding whether a move is needed

The National Institute on Aging says the decision about whether and when an older adult should move is often difficult and emotional. Learn the housing options and talk with your family about the pros and cons before making a decision. If a move becomes necessary, look for transition help from health care professionals, a local senior center, or the Area Agency on Aging.

What this means for you. You do not have to decide the setting and the moving date in the same conversation. Start with the official options, then look at the practical steps.

Source: National Institute on Aging, aging in place. Source: National Institute on Aging, what is long-term care.

04Settling in after a move

The National Institute on Aging notes that moving an older adult to an assisted living facility can be stressful and is a big change, and that it may take some time for everyone to adjust. Official suggestions include getting to know the staff before the move, being an advocate for the person’s needs and preferences, being supportive and listening, sharing your own feelings with a social worker or mental health professional, checking in regularly after the move, and building a relationship with staff. If something about care or rights is wrong, you can also contact the Florida Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program at 1-888-831-0404.

What this means for you. A hard first week does not automatically mean the move was a mistake. Stay in contact with staff, and use the Ombudsman if a right is being ignored.

Source: National Institute on Aging. Source: Florida Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program.

06Deciding whether a move is needed

Most people prefer to stay in their own home for as long as possible. The National Institute on Aging says there may come a time when it is no longer safe or comfortable to live alone. One person may decide a move is right because they cannot or do not want to manage the home any longer. For another, the need for regular, hands-on care motivates a change. Learn as much as you can about the housing options, and talk with your family about the pros and cons of each option before making a decision. If a move becomes necessary, look for resources to help with the transition, such as counseling and support, guidance from health care professionals, and suggestions from a local senior center or Area Agency on Aging.

Source: National Institute on Aging, aging in place. Source: National Institute on Aging, what is long-term care.

07Steps of a move into assisted living

The official steps below come from Florida law and federal guidance. For help with movers or other practical details, contact me to go over which ones are best for you.

08Step by step

  1. 01

    Talk through the options

    Find out about available housing options and discuss them together. Some families use a meeting or a call to talk through the pros and cons, with the goal of a plan that works for everyone, especially the older person. National Institute on Aging.

  2. 02

    Check that the facility is licensed in Florida

    A license from AHCA is required to operate an assisted living facility in Florida. Ask to see the current license or look the community up on FloridaHealthFinder. Florida AHCA; Florida Statutes s. 429.07.

  3. 03

    Complete the medical examination

    The owner or administrator decides whether someone is appropriate for that facility, using a medical examination, the person’s needs, the services the facility can provide, and the limits of its license. Each resident must be examined within 60 days before admission or within 30 days after admission. Florida Statutes s. 429.26.

  4. 04

    Read the written contract before you sign

    Florida requires a written contract at or before admission, and you should receive a copy. It must list the services and accommodations, the rates or charges, at least 30 days’ written notice of a rate increase, and a refund policy if the resident transfers, is discharged, or dies. AHCA also says the contract should cover housing, optional services, and discharge and bed-hold policies. Florida Statutes s. 429.24; Florida AHCA.

  5. 05

    Ask for help with the practical side of the move

    Contact me to go over which ones are best for you. The National Institute on Aging also points families to local senior centers and the Area Agency on Aging for transition help. National Institute on Aging.

09A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Look the community up on FloridaHealthFinder and ask to see the current license.
  • Ask how the admission assessment and medical exam work.
  • Read the contract’s services, rates, 30-day increase notice, refund, discharge, and bed-hold sections.
