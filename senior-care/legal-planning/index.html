Legal & Planning

A plain explanation of Florida powers of attorney, health care surrogates, and advance directives, with official forms and links. This is not legal advice.

Jeff15 years in senior care

Quick answer
  • Florida law on health care advance directives is in Chapter 765, Florida Statutes. Powers of attorney are in Chapter 709. Florida Statutes, Chapter 765; Florida Statutes, Chapter 709.
  • A health care surrogate is someone you name to make medical decisions if you cannot speak for yourself. A suggested statutory form is posted with FloridaHealthFinder materials.
  • This page explains official documents only. It is not legal advice. Consider talking with an attorney about your own situation.

01This is not legal advice

The summaries below are for general information, taken from official Florida sources. They are not a substitute for advice from a licensed attorney, and they are not a complete review of the law. Forms should be completed carefully; some must be signed and witnessed as the statute requires.

02Power of attorney

Florida’s power of attorney law is in Chapter 709 of the Florida Statutes, including rules for durable powers of attorney. A durable power of attorney can let someone you name act for you on financial or legal matters if you are alive but unable to handle those matters yourself. Read the statute, and consider legal advice, before you sign anything.

Source: Florida Statutes, Chapter 709. Source: The Florida Bar, consumer information on advance directives.

03Health care surrogate and living will

Chapter 765 of the Florida Statutes covers health care advance directives, including living wills and the designation of a health care surrogate. A suggested form for designating a health care surrogate, revised October 2021 and based on s. 765.203, Florida Statutes, is available from FloridaHealthFinder materials. The Florida Bar’s consumer page also points to AHCA’s Health Care Advance Directives consumer guide and forms. Give copies to your health care provider and to the person you name.

Source: Florida Statutes, Chapter 765. Source: Florida Designation of Health Care Surrogate (suggested form). Source: The Florida Bar.

A short checklist
  • Read the official statute or form before you sign.
  • Ask an attorney if you have questions about your own documents.
  • Give copies to your health care provider and to the person you name.
