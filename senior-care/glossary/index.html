01Glossary of senior care terms

Assisted living
In Florida, a licensed facility that provides housing, meals, and one or more personal services for more than 24 hours, for people who need help but not 24-hour nursing. Florida Statutes s. 429.02; Florida AHCA.
Independent living
Generally for people who can manage on their own and want a maintenance-free home and social life, without daily personal care. National Institute on Aging.
Memory care
Specialized support for Alzheimer’s disease or another dementia. It may be offered inside an assisted living community, and it is not defined the same way everywhere. Alzheimer's Association.
Skilled nursing facility (SNF)
A Medicare-certified facility that can provide short-term skilled nursing or therapy. Medicare may cover a stay when you qualify. Medicare.gov.
Nursing home / custodial care
Ongoing help with daily activities. Medicare generally does not cover long-term custodial nursing home care. Medicare.gov.
Medicaid SMMC Long-Term Care
Florida’s Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care program, which can include assisted living for people who qualify. Florida AHCA.
CARES
Comprehensive Assessment and Review for Long-Term Care Services, the Florida Department of Elder Affairs program that assesses medical eligibility for Medicaid long-term care. Florida CARES.
ADRC / Elder Helpline
Aging and Disability Resource Centers, reached statewide at 1-800-963-5337, for screening and local long-term care resources. Florida Department of Elder Affairs.
OSS
Optional State Supplementation, a DCF monthly cash supplement for eligible people in certain licensed residential settings, including assisted living. Florida DCF.
Aid and Attendance
A VA addition to a pension for qualified Veterans and survivors who need help with daily activities or meet other official rules. U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs.
Long-Term Care Ombudsman
A Florida program that advocates for people living in assisted living facilities, nursing homes, and adult family care homes. Florida LTCOP.
SHINE
Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders, Florida’s official Medicare counseling program, reached through the Elder Helpline. Florida SHINE.

Official sources

Official state & federal resources

These are public government or national organization pages. Use them to check a fact or start an application. Call or email Jeff if you want help making sense of them.

  • Florida AHCA / FloridaHealthFinder, assisted living guide

    Florida’s official consumer guide to assisted living, including how to look up a facility’s license and compare communities.

  • Florida Statutes, Chapter 429 (Assisted Care Communities)

    The Florida law that governs assisted living facilities, including contracts, resident rights, and licenses.

  • Florida Medicaid SMMC Long-Term Care FAQs

    Official answers about screening, the waitlist, enrollment, and services, including assisted living.

  • Medicare.gov

    The official U.S. site for Medicare coverage, costs, and eligibility. Phone: 1-800-633-4227

  • VA Aid and Attendance and Housebound allowance

    How Veterans and surviving spouses who already receive a VA pension may apply for a monthly Aid and Attendance or Housebound addition.

  • Florida Elder Helpline and Aging and Disability Resource Centers

    The statewide starting point for screening and local long-term care resources. Collier County is listed on the official Elder Helpline page as 239-652-6901. Phone: 1-800-96-ELDER (1-800-963-5337)

  • Florida Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program

    A state program that advocates for people living in Florida assisted living facilities, nursing homes, and adult family care homes. Phone: 1-888-831-0404

  • Florida SHINE (Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders)

    Florida’s official Medicare counseling program, reached through the Elder Helpline. Phone: 1-800-963-5337
