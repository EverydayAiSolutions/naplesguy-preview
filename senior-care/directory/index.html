Naples · Senior Care

Assisted living & nursing homes in
Naples, FL

This is a neutral list of every currently licensed assisted living facility, nursing home, adult day care center, and adult family care home in Naples and nearby Bonita Springs, Estero, and Fort Myers, taken from Florida’s official license records. Life plan (continuing care) communities listed by the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation are included as a separate type. Nothing here is a recommendation, a ranking, or a review.

Licenses and ratings change. Confirm the current license, inspections, and any Medicaid participation on the official profile before you decide. Related guides: Assisted Living, Memory Care, and Skilled Nursing & Rehab.

How to use this list

What each license means

  • Assisted living facility. A Florida-licensed place where people can live and get help with daily activities, such as bathing, dressing, or medication reminders, but not 24-hour nursing, except as a specialty license allows.
  • Extended Congregate Care (ECC). A specialty license that lets some assisted living facilities provide a higher level of care, so a resident may be able to stay as needs increase. Florida does not issue a separate “memory care” license. The Memory care filter on this page shows facilities with a state ECC license, ECC beds, or a Specialized Alzheimer’s Services license.
  • Limited Nursing Services. A specialty license that lets an assisted living facility provide a limited set of nursing services through licensed nurses. It is not the same as a nursing home.
  • Limited Mental Health. A specialty license for serving residents who have a mental health diagnosis and meet Florida’s rules for that specialty.
  • Nursing home, and hospital skilled nursing unit. Licensed or federally certified places that provide 24-hour nursing care and short-term rehab.
  • Adult day care. A licensed daytime program. It is not a place to live.
  • Adult family care home. A licensed private home that provides room, board, and personal care for a small number of adults.
  • Life plan (continuing care) community. Listed by the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation. A contract may include independent living and later care on the same campus. Assisted living or nursing care on that campus, if separately licensed, also appears under those types.
  • Provisional license. Florida sometimes issues a provisional license, often after a change of ownership or for a new facility, while a standard license is pending. The official profile has the current dates.

How to read an inspection report

  • Open the facility’s official FloridaHealthFinder profile, linked on each card as “State profile & inspections.”
  • Review inspection results and any deficiencies, and look at whether a plan of correction was submitted.
  • For nursing homes, also use Medicare Care Compare. CMS gives each nursing home a star rating from 1 to 5. CMS describes 5 stars as much above average quality and 1 star as much below average. Those ratings are assigned by Medicare, not by this website. How CMS explains the ratings.
  • If you have a concern about care or residents’ rights, contact the Florida Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program at 1-888-831-0404.
  • For a broader starting point, call the Florida Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337. The official county list also has Collier County at 239-652-6901.
