01Help at home

Quick answer
  • Home-based care can include personal help with everyday activities, household chores, meals, medications, and health care. It may be provided by family or by paid caregivers. National Institute on Aging.
  • Medicare covers certain part-time or intermittent home health services if you are eligible and homebound, ordered by a provider, and served by a Medicare-certified agency. Medicare.gov, home health services.
  • Medicare does not pay for 24-hour-a-day care at home, home meal delivery, or homemaker services unrelated to your care plan, or for custodial care alone. Medicare.gov, home health services.

02Help at home, and aging in place

The National Institute on Aging describes home-based care as personal help with everyday activities, household chores, meals, medications, and health care. It may be provided by family or by paid caregivers. Many older adults want to stay in their own home; whether that is possible depends on the help available and on health needs.

What this means for you. Staying at home is a plan, not an automatic result. If daily help is already needed, compare home support with licensed assisted living so the choice is conscious.

Source: National Institute on Aging. Source: National Institute on Aging, what is long-term care.

03Medicare home health, and help at home in assisted living

Covered Medicare home health services can include medically necessary part-time or intermittent skilled nursing, therapy, medical social services, and, when you are also getting skilled care, part-time home health aide help. You must be eligible and homebound, have services ordered by a provider, and use a Medicare-certified agency. You pay nothing for covered home health services; after the Part B deductible you pay 20% for covered medical equipment. Medicare does not pay for 24-hour-a-day care at home, home meal delivery, or homemaker services unrelated to your care plan, or for custodial care alone.

Florida AHCA also says a resident of an assisted living facility may often contract with a licensed home health agency if they do not need 24-hour nursing, and they must still meet the facility’s residency rules.

What this means for you. Medicare home health is skilled, part-time care with eligibility rules — not a standing homemaker service. If you are comparing agencies, Medicare’s Care Compare can help you find certified providers.

Source: Medicare.gov, home health services. Source: Florida AHCA. Source: Medicare Care Compare.

05Respite care

Quick answer
  • Respite care provides short-term relief for primary caregivers, giving them time to rest, travel, or spend time with other family and friends. National Institute on Aging.
  • It may last from a few hours to several weeks, and it can take place at home, in a health care facility, or at an adult day care center. National Institute on Aging.
  • For a person receiving hospice care, Medicare will cover most of the cost for up to five consecutive days of respite care in a hospital or skilled nursing facility. Medicaid also may provide payment assistance. National Institute on Aging.

06What respite care is

The National Institute on Aging says respite care provides short-term relief for primary caregivers, so they can rest, travel, or spend time with other family and friends. It may last from a few hours to several weeks, and it can take place at home, in a health care facility, or at an adult day care center. In some cases family, friends, or volunteers can step in; professionals also offer these services. The ongoing demands of caregiving can strain even the more resilient person, which is why NIA says it is critical for caregivers to ask for help and take a break.

What this means for you. Respite is planned short-term coverage so the usual caregiver can step away — not a permanent placement decision by itself.

Source: National Institute on Aging.

07How families sometimes pay

The National Institute on Aging says most private health insurance plans do not cover the costs of respite care, though some long-term care insurance plans may. You must pay all costs not covered by insurance or government programs. For a person receiving hospice care, Medicare will cover most of the cost for up to five consecutive days of respite care in a hospital or skilled nursing facility. Medicaid also may provide payment assistance.

What this means for you. Ask the insurer or program what is covered before you book a stay. Do not assume a private health plan will pay.

Source: National Institute on Aging.

What this means for you. These official numbers are the place to ask what short-term relief is actually available near Naples this month, including adult day programs.

11Respite and support for caregivers

NIA describes respite as short-term relief so a primary caregiver can rest, travel, or spend time with other family and friends. In some cases family, friends, or volunteers can step in; professionals also offer these services. For a person receiving hospice care, Medicare will cover most of the cost for up to five consecutive days of respite in a hospital or skilled nursing facility. Medicaid may also help. Most private health insurance plans do not cover respite, according to NIA.

A detailed official symptom list for caregiver burnout was not confirmed on the NIA or other approved government pages fetched for this update, so this page does not invent one. If caregiving feels like too much, official starting points are the Elder Helpline, the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116, respite care, and NAMI’s family and caregiver resources.

What this means for you. Asking for a short break is part of keeping care going. Start with an official local number rather than waiting until a crisis.

FAQ

Questions families often ask

Does Medicare pay for full-time home care in Naples?

No. Medicare can cover certain part-time or intermittent home health services if a person is eligible and homebound, but Medicare.gov says it does not pay for 24-hour-a-day care at home or for custodial care alone. Private non-medical home care is usually paid out of pocket, unless another program or insurance applies.

What does in-home care cost, compared with a move?

The CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025, collected from July through November 2025, lists a Florida median of $32 an hour for a non-medical caregiver, or $6,101 a month at 44 hours a week. That is not a 24-hour quote. The same survey lists a Florida median of $5,610 a month for an assisted living community. Compare those official figures with written local quotes on the cost guide and the home care versus moving page.

When should a family look at moving instead of more hours at home?

The National Institute on Aging says most people prefer to stay at home as long as possible, and that there may come a time when it is no longer safe or comfortable to live alone. Compare the hours of help actually needed, whether that help is available, and what a licensed community would include. Jeff can go over both paths one-on-one.
