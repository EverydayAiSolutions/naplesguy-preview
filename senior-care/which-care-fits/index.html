01Which type of care may fit

Quick answer
  • Assisted living, independent living, memory care, home care, and skilled nursing are different kinds of help. Official sources describe who each one is for.
  • This quiz is not a medical or financial assessment. It only points you to the page that matches what you select.

02The official differences, in brief

Independent living is generally for people who can manage on their own. Assisted living is for people who need help with daily care but not 24-hour nursing. Memory care is specialized support for Alzheimer’s disease or another dementia and may be offered inside an assisted living community. Home-based care can be personal help from family or paid caregivers, or Medicare-covered home health when you qualify. Nursing homes focus more on medical care, and Medicare may cover short-term skilled nursing and rehab when you meet the rules — not long-term custodial care.

What this means for you. Use the quiz and chart as a map to the right guide, then confirm details on the official pages linked there. The quiz does not replace a medical exam or a Florida license check.

06A first-week plan

Quick answer
  • Start by noticing signs that more help may be needed, then talk with the older adult and with family. National Institute on Aging.
  • Call Florida’s Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337 to reach your Aging and Disability Resource Center. Florida Department of Elder Affairs.
  • Look up any Florida facility on FloridaHealthFinder before you tour. Florida AHCA.
  • If someone is in the hospital, ask whether the stay is inpatient, because observation time does not count toward Medicare’s usual three-day skilled nursing rule. Medicare.gov.

09Independent living

Quick answer
  • Independent living is generally for people who can manage on their own and want a maintenance-free home and social life, without daily personal care. National Institute on Aging.
  • Assisted living is for people who need help with daily care but do not need nursing home-level medical care. National Institute on Aging.
  • In Florida, a licensed assisted living facility is defined as a place that provides housing, meals, and one or more personal services for more than 24 hours. Florida Statutes s. 429.02.

10Independent living compared with assisted living

Independent living is generally for people who can manage on their own and want a maintenance-free home and social life, without daily personal care such as bathing, dressing, or medication help. Assisted living is for people who need that daily support but do not need the level of medical care in a nursing home.

Some larger organizations also offer more than one level of care, such as independent living and skilled nursing, but exact arrangements vary by facility and by state. Marketing language can be broader than a license. In Florida, a licensed assisted living facility is defined as a place that provides housing, meals, and one or more personal services for more than 24 hours. If a community is advertising personal care, look it up on FloridaHealthFinder and ask which AHCA license it holds.

11When more help may be needed

The National Institute on Aging suggests spending time in the home and watching for signs such as trouble managing medications, changes around the house, significant weight change, mood changes, or memory and decision-making problems that go beyond ordinary forgetfulness. Those signs do not, by themselves, decide the setting, but they are a reason to compare options.

What this means for you. If those signs are showing up, use the official checklist on Is it time? and the comparison on Which care fits? before you sign a contract.

12After a hospital discharge

Quick answer
  • For Original Medicare, you generally must be admitted as an inpatient for at least three days in a row, not counting the discharge day, before Medicare will cover a skilled nursing facility stay. Medicare.gov.
  • Time in the emergency room or under observation does not count toward those three days. Medicare.gov.
  • You generally must enter the SNF within 30 days of leaving the hospital. Medicare.gov.

13Hospital-discharge rush checklist

When discharge is happening quickly, these official Medicare points are the ones to confirm with the hospital team:

  • Ask whether the hospital stay is inpatient or observation. Observation time, even overnight, does not count toward the three-day inpatient stay Original Medicare usually requires for skilled nursing coverage.
  • Count the admission day but not the discharge day when you ask whether there has been a qualifying three-day inpatient stay.
  • Ask whether Medicare is expected to cover a skilled nursing facility stay, and whether the facility is Medicare-certified.
  • Ask about the timing: you generally must enter the SNF within 30 days of leaving the hospital.
  • If hospital status was changed to outpatient observation, ask about appealing a related denial of Part A hospital or SNF coverage.
  • If you have a Medicare Advantage plan, ask whether it waives the three-day rule and what copays apply.
  • For Original Medicare in 2026, Medicare.gov lists $0 per day for days 1–20 after the Part A deductible, $217 per day for days 21–100, and all costs after day 100 in a benefit period. Check your plan if you have Medicare Advantage.

Source: Medicare.gov, skilled nursing facility care. Source: Medicare.gov, 2026 costs.

14How to choose a nursing home in Naples

A neutral list of state-licensed nursing homes and hospital skilled nursing units in Naples and nearby, with each facility’s official FloridaHealthFinder profile and Medicare star rating, is on the Senior Living Directory.

If you are choosing a nursing home or rehab facility in Naples after a hospital stay, start with official tools. Medicare’s Care Compare lets you find and compare Medicare-certified nursing homes. CMS’s Five-Star Quality Rating System, shown on Care Compare, gives each nursing home a rating of 1 to 5 stars; CMS says 5 stars means much above average quality and 1 star means quality much below average. Take Medicare’s official nursing home visit checklist with you. The National Institute on Aging suggests asking about costs and special needs, visiting more than once, including at mealtime, and looking for Medicare and Medicaid certification, state licensing, accessibility, residents who look well cared for, respectful staff, and a clean, well-maintained facility. Read the contract carefully. FloridaHealthFinder can show Florida license and complaint information. If you have a concern about a Florida facility, AHCA’s Consumer Complaint Call Center is 1-888-419-3456, and the Southwest District of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman (serving Collier County) is 239-338-2563. You can also call Jeff to talk through the next step.

15A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Confirm inpatient versus observation status in writing if you can.
  • Ask if a three-day qualifying inpatient stay has been met for Original Medicare.
  • Ask whether the recommended SNF is Medicare-certified.
  • Look the facility up on Care Compare and FloridaHealthFinder.

FAQ

Questions families often ask

What is independent living, compared with assisted living in Florida?

The National Institute on Aging describes independent living as generally for people who can manage on their own and want a maintenance-free home and social life, without daily personal care. Assisted living is for people who need help with daily care, such as bathing, dressing, medications, or supervision, but do not need the level of medical care in a nursing home. In Florida, a licensed assisted living facility is defined as a place that provides housing, meals, and one or more personal services for more than 24 hours.

Where can families look at independent living in Naples?

The Senior Living Directory includes independent living and life-plan listings from official Florida records, alongside licensed assisted living. Life-plan or continuing-care communities are also mapped by the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation. Town placement pages for North Naples, the City of Naples, East Naples, and nearby cities list nearby hospitals and license counts.

What if care needs change after a move to independent living?

Ask what happens if a person later needs help with daily activities, and whether assisted living, memory care, or skilled nursing is available on the same campus or nearby. Look up any licensed level of care on FloridaHealthFinder. Jeff can help the family compare staying at home with help versus moving, and can help with a house sale if that becomes part of the plan.
