02Assisted living cost in Naples and across Florida

Families often search for an assisted living cost in Naples and find that there is no single local number. Florida AHCA says fees are not the same everywhere, and a community may charge a basic fee plus extra amounts for added services. The CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025, collected from July through November 2025, lists a Florida median of $5,610 a month, or $67,320 a year. Ask each community for a current written rate.

Source: Florida AHCA. Source: CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025.

03What is included, and can the rate go up?

Pricing is not always all-inclusive. AHCA describes typical basic services, but a facility may charge extra for additional services. The contract must list the services and accommodations and the rates or charges, and it must provide at least 30 days’ written notice of a rate increase. A new service that was not previously charged does not require that 30-day notice. Florida does not set a standard move-in or community fee; ask for a written explanation of any one-time fees, deposits, or advance payments, and how refunds work, before you sign.

Source: Florida AHCA. Source: Florida Statutes s. 429.24.

04Does Medicare pay for assisted living?

Medicare does not pay for assisted living. The National Institute on Aging notes that people usually pay the full cost themselves, though Medicaid may cover some aspects depending on the state, and some long-term care insurance policies may help. In Florida, AHCA says payment is usually from private funds, and you should ask whether a community accepts long-term care insurance, veterans benefits, or Optional State Supplementation.

Source: National Institute on Aging. Source: Florida AHCA.

05Does Medicaid pay for assisted living in Florida?

It can, for people who qualify for Florida’s Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care program, which lists assisted living among its services. Start by calling your local Aging and Disability Resource Center for a screening; you may be placed on a waitlist. When enrollment is available, the Department of Elder Affairs CARES program assesses medical eligibility (generally a nursing home level of care), and the Department of Children and Families determines financial eligibility. For general questions, AHCA points families to the statewide Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337 (1-800-96-ELDER).

Read the full official path, including 2026 income and asset figures as stated by AHCA, on the Florida Medicaid for senior care page.

Source: Florida AHCA, SMMC Long-Term Care FAQs. Source: Florida CARES.

06VA benefits, long-term care insurance, and OSS

Besides private pay, Florida AHCA says to ask whether a facility accepts long-term care insurance, veterans benefits, or Optional State Supplementation (OSS), and to look for OSS-eligible facilities on FloridaHealthFinder. Some long-term care insurance policies help with assisted living, but coverage depends on the policy. Veterans or surviving spouses who already receive a VA pension and need help with daily activities, are housebound, or are in a nursing home because of disability-related loss of ability, may qualify for VA Aid and Attendance or a Housebound allowance; you apply through the VA.

Source: Florida AHCA. Source: U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. Source: Florida DCF, on OSS.

07A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Ask for a written list of the monthly fee, what is included, and what costs extra.
  • Ask about any move-in or community fee, deposits, and the refund policy.
  • Read how and when the monthly rate can go up (at least 30 days’ written notice in Florida, with a limited exception).
  • Ask whether the community accepts Medicaid Long-Term Care, VA benefits, long-term care insurance, or OSS.

09Ways families often pay

People often use more than one source: personal funds, government programs such as Medicare, Medicaid, or VA benefits, and private financing such as long-term care insurance. What is covered depends on the program and on whether the person is eligible.

Florida AHCA says fees for assisted living are set by each provider and are not the same everywhere. A community may charge a basic fee plus extra amounts for added services. The CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025, collected from July through November 2025, lists a Florida median of $5,610 a month, or $67,320 a year, for an assisted living community. Local prices in Naples and Collier County can be higher or lower, so ask each community for a current written rate.

What this means for you. Start with a written rate from the community you are looking at, then see which official program — if any — might apply. The median is a statewide benchmark, not a Naples quote.

Source: National Institute on Aging. Source: Florida AHCA. Source: CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025.

10What Medicare does and does not pay

Medicare does not pay for assisted living. The National Institute on Aging notes that people usually pay the full cost themselves, though Medicaid may cover some aspects depending on the state, and some long-term care insurance policies may help.

Medicare can cover short-term skilled nursing facility care when you qualify — generally after a qualifying inpatient hospital stay, in a Medicare-certified facility, when you need skilled care. For Original Medicare in 2026, Medicare.gov lists $0 per day for days 1–20 after the Part A deductible, $217 per day for days 21–100, and all costs after day 100 in a benefit period. Medicare generally does not cover long-term custodial nursing home care. Medicare home health is separate: it can cover certain part-time or intermittent services if you are eligible and homebound, but it does not pay for 24-hour-a-day care at home or for custodial care alone.

What this means for you. If the need is ongoing help with daily activities in assisted living, do not count on Medicare to pay the monthly fee. If the need is short-term rehab after a hospital stay, start with the skilled nursing guide and confirm coverage with the hospital team.

Read more: Skilled Nursing & Rehab and Home Care.

Source: National Institute on Aging. Source: Medicare.gov, nursing home care. Source: Medicare.gov, skilled nursing facility care. Source: Medicare.gov, 2026 costs. Source: Medicare.gov, home health services.

11Florida Medicaid long-term care

Florida’s Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care program lists assisted living among its services, for people who qualify. Start by calling your local Aging and Disability Resource Center for a screening; you may be placed on a waitlist. When enrollment is available, the Department of Elder Affairs CARES program assesses medical eligibility (generally a nursing home level of care), and the Department of Children and Families determines financial eligibility. For general questions, AHCA points families to the statewide Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337. In Collier County, the official statewide Elder Helpline page lists 239-652-6901.

What this means for you. Medicaid is not automatic, and there can be a waitlist. If this path might apply, use the detailed Medicaid guide and call the Elder Helpline rather than guessing at the limits.

The step-by-step path, 2026 income and asset figures as stated by AHCA, Optional State Supplementation, and official tools for finding participating settings are on Does Medicaid pay for assisted living in Florida?

Source: Florida AHCA, SMMC Long-Term Care FAQs. Source: Florida Dept of Elder Affairs, CARES. Source: Elder Helpline, by county.

17Medicaid in Florida

$2,982/mo

2026 monthly income limit for a non-working individual in included Florida Medicaid waivers, including the Long-Term Care Waiver, as stated by AHCA (300% of the Federal Benefit Rate). Couple: $5,964. These figures can change.

$2,000

2026 asset limit for a non-working individual in those same included waivers, as stated by AHCA. Couple: $3,000. These figures can change.

Quick answer
  • Florida’s Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care program lists assisted living among its services for people who qualify. Florida AHCA, SMMC Long-Term Care FAQs.
  • To receive Long-Term Care services, a person must be 65 or older and eligible for Medicaid, or 18 or older and eligible for Medicaid due to a disability, and be determined by CARES to need a nursing home level of care. Florida AHCA.
  • DCF determines financial eligibility. The Department of Elder Affairs CARES program determines medical eligibility. Florida AHCA.
  • An AHCA fact sheet for 2026 states that non-working individuals in included waivers, including the Long-Term Care Waiver, have a monthly income limit of $2,982 for an individual or $5,964 for a couple, and an asset limit of $2,000 for an individual or $3,000 for a couple. Those figures can change. AHCA, Working People with Disabilities FAQ 2026.

18Does Medicaid pay for assisted living in Florida?

It can, for people who qualify for the Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care program. AHCA says persons may receive Long-Term Care program services if they are age 65 and over and eligible for Medicaid, or age 18 and over and eligible for Medicaid due to a disability, and if CARES at the Department of Elder Affairs determines they are at a nursing home level of care (or a hospital level of care for persons with cystic fibrosis). Two state agencies decide: DOEA decides medical eligibility, and DCF decides financial eligibility.

Source: Florida AHCA, who can receive long-term care. Source: Florida AHCA, SMMC FAQs.

19Income and asset limits, as stated for 2026

An official AHCA FAQ, labeled for 2026, says that non-working individuals in included waivers — including the Long-Term Care Waiver — have a monthly income limit of 300% of the Federal Benefit Rate. In 2026, that equals $2,982 for an individual or $5,964 for a couple, and an asset limit of $2,000 for an individual or $3,000 for a couple. The same document describes higher limits for certain working people in those waivers. These figures can change, and DCF makes the financial decision for each applicant. If income is over the usual long-term care limit, DCF’s July 2025 SSI-Related Medicaid fact sheet describes a Qualified Income Trust as one tool that may be considered; that is a legal matter, not advice from this site.

Source: AHCA, Working People with Disabilities FAQ 2026. Source: Florida DCF, Medicaid.

20How to apply

Start with a screening through your local Aging and Disability Resource Center or the Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337. AHCA’s “become eligible” page describes the path after release from the waitlist: Form 3008, a Medicaid application processed by DCF, and an in-person CARES assessment after the completed form is returned. You may apply for Florida public assistance, including Medicaid, through DCF’s ACCESS portal. AHCA says you may also call DCF at 1-866-762-2237 for more information about Medicaid.

Source: Florida AHCA, become eligible. Source: DCF ACCESS Florida. Source: Florida Elder Helpline. Source: Florida CARES.

21Waitlist and enrollment

AHCA’s Long-Term Care recipient FAQs and “become eligible” page describe a waitlist (also called a pre-enrollment list). When enrollment is available, the ADRC contacts the person. If financial and medical eligibility are met, AHCA sends a welcome packet with information about the program and how to select a plan.

Source: Florida AHCA, SMMC FAQs. Source: Florida AHCA, become eligible.

22Optional State Supplementation (OSS)

OSS is a separate DCF program. DCF says it provides monthly cash payments to indigent elderly or disabled individuals who live in special non-institutional residential living facilities, including assisted living facilities, adult family care homes, and mental health residential treatment facilities. To qualify, an individual must need assistance with activities of daily living. The program provides a monthly check that supplements the individual’s income so they can pay the facility a provider rate established by DCF. Florida AHCA also tells families to ask whether a community accepts OSS and to look for OSS-eligible facilities on FloridaHealthFinder. This page does not state a current OSS payment amount, because an official 2026 dollar figure was not confirmed on the DCF or AHCA pages fetched for this update.

Source: Florida DCF, Temporary Cash Assistance (OSS description). Source: Florida AHCA.

23Find Naples communities that accept Medicaid

This site does not publish a list of individual communities. Participation can change, so use the official tools below and then confirm with the facility and with the Medicaid plan.

    Open Care Compare, choose nursing homes, and search for Naples, Florida, to compare Medicare-certified nursing homes. This federal tool does not replace a Florida license check on FloridaHealthFinder.

  • AHCA Long-Term Care plans for Collier County (Region F)

    In February 2025, AHCA moved from numbered regions to lettered regions A–I. Under Florida Statutes s. 409.966 (2026), Collier County is in Region F, which is the region that was previously called Region 8. Open AHCA’s current plan poster (dated February 4, 2025) for the plans awarded in Region F. Choice counselors are available at 1-877-711-3662, and AHCA also points people to flmedicaidmanagedcare.com to enroll.

25VA benefits

Quick answer
  • As a Veteran, you may be able to get health care at home or while in an assisted living or residential facility, if you meet VA’s requirements. U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs.
  • Access generally requires that you are signed up for VA health care, that VA concludes you need the service, and that the service or space is available near you. U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs.
  • Veterans or surviving spouses who already receive a VA pension may also qualify for Aid and Attendance or a Housebound allowance. VA Aid and Attendance.

26VA long-term care services

VA says its long-term care services can include 24/7 nursing and medical care, physical therapy, help with daily tasks, comfort care, and support for caregivers who need a break. Care settings may include nursing homes, assisted-living centers, certain private homes, adult day health centers, and a Veteran’s own home. To find out how to access these services, VA says to contact your VA social worker, or apply for VA health care if you are not already enrolled. Some services are covered under standard VA health benefits, sometimes with a copay; other services may be paid through Medicaid, Medicare, or private insurance.

Source: VA long-term care benefits.

27Aid and Attendance, and how to apply

VA Aid and Attendance or Housebound benefits provide monthly payments added to a VA pension for qualified Veterans and survivors. You may be eligible for Aid and Attendance if you already get a VA pension and you need help with daily activities, must spend a large portion of the day in bed, are a patient in a nursing home because of disability-related loss of ability, or have limited eyesight as described on the VA page. Housebound benefits have a separate rule, and you cannot get both at the same time. You can apply online, by mail, or in person using the forms and addresses on the official VA page.

Source: VA Aid and Attendance and Housebound allowance.
