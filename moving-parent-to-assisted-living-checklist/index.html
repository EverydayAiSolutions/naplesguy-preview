Checklist for Moving a Parent to Assisted Living

This checklist for moving a parent to assisted living walks a Naples family through each step: choosing the community, Florida's required paperwork, the contract, packing, move day and the first weeks after.

Stage 1

Before the move: choosing and signing

Choose the community

  • Agree as a family on the type of care: independent living, assisted living or memory care.
  • Check each license and recent inspection on FloridaHealthFinder.
  • Tour at least two communities, once at a mealtime.
  • Ask for written pricing, care levels and one-time fees.

Paperwork Florida requires

  • Schedule the medical examination within 60 days before move-in (or within 30 days after), commonly on AHCA Form 1823.
  • Read the contract: services, rates, 30-day notice of increases and the refund policy (section 429.24).
  • Gather the health care surrogate designation, durable power of attorney and any living will.
  • Copy insurance and Medicare cards, and a current medication list.

Stage 2

Packing and downsizing

Measure first

  • Get the apartment floor plan and measure the favorite chair, bed and dresser.
  • Ask what furniture the community supplies and what is not allowed.
  • Arrange the room so the bed, chair and lamp match the old routine.

What to bring

  • Two weeks of labeled clothing and comfortable shoes.
  • Familiar bedding, family photos, a clock and a calendar.
  • Glasses, hearing aids, dentures and spare batteries.
  • A simple phone and charger, and a list of family numbers.

The rest of the house

Families usually sort the house into keep, family, sell or donate, and estate sale. Downsizing in Naples covers the order to do it in.

Stage 3

Move day

Morning

  • Bring medications in original pharmacy containers, plus the written list.
  • Hand the wellness staff the medical exam form and doctor contacts.
  • Set up the bedroom first, so it is ready before evening.

Afternoon and evening

  • Have lunch together in the dining room.
  • Meet the staff on the evening shift.
  • Agree on when family will call or visit next, and keep the first goodbye short and warm.

Stage 4

The first 30 days

Care

  • Review the service plan with the staff in the first weeks.
  • Confirm medications match the doctor's orders.
  • Ask how changes in health will be reported to family.

Home and money

  • Forward mail and update the address with Social Security, banks and insurers.
  • Decide whether to sell, rent or keep the house.
  • Keep the contract and every bill in one folder.

Good to know

Questions people ask

What paperwork is needed to move into assisted living in Florida?

Florida requires a medical examination by a physician, physician assistant or advanced practice registered nurse within 60 days before admission or within 30 days after (section 429.26). Communities commonly record it on AHCA Form 1823. Expect a signed resident contract, a medication list, insurance cards and copies of health care and financial powers of attorney.

What must a Florida assisted living contract include?

Under section 429.24, the contract must spell out services, accommodations and rates, give at least 30 days' written notice of a rate increase, and include a refund policy. A resident cannot be required to give more than 30 days' notice to end the contract.

What should a parent bring to assisted living?

Familiar bedding, a favorite chair, family photos, labeled clothing for two weeks, toiletries, glasses and hearing aids with spare batteries, medications in original pharmacy containers, and a simple phone. Measure the apartment first; most are far smaller than the house.

What happens to the house when a parent moves?

Some families sell, some rent and some keep it for a spouse. Jeff Silberman is a licensed Florida real estate agent and can walk through the options, timing and costs of selling the house.
