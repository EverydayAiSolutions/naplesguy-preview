Selling Your Home

What Florida sellers should expect in Naples and Collier County: required disclosures, the contract, inspections, flood zones, homestead and portability, and HOA or condo rules, from official sources.

01Steps to selling in Florida

Quick answer
  • Florida sales typically use a written contract that states the price, closing date, and who pays which costs.
  • Sellers of homes in an HOA or condominium have document duties under Florida law. Official estoppel-certificate fees are capped, and the association has 10 business days to issue the certificate.
  • A homestead exemption does not automatically follow you. File again in person in Collier County, and use official form DR-501T if you want to transfer Save Our Homes portability.

02Before you list

Gather what a buyer and closing agent will need: a copy of your deed, your latest tax bill from the Collier County Tax Collector, any HOA or condo documents, information you will disclose about known problems, and information about flood claims if the property has a flood history. Florida condo and HOA statutes require certain documents and disclosures, explained in HOA & Condo Rules.

If you still have a mortgage, ask your lender for a payoff quote as you near closing. Payoff figures change with interest, so use a current quote. Look up your own parcel on the Collier County Property Appraiser site so you know the current assessed value and exemptions.

Source: Collier Tax Collector. Source: Collier Property Appraiser. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.503 (2026).

What this means for you. A little preparation — deed, tax bill, association contacts — saves time when a Naples offer comes in. If the home is in an HOA or a condo, start the document request early; buyers have short statutory review windows.

03From offer to closing

  1. 01

    Review each offer against the contract form

    Look at price, deposits, requested repairs or credits, closing date, and who pays title insurance and documentary stamp tax. Those items are negotiated in the contract. Florida tax law does not automatically assign the deed stamps to the seller, even though many contracts do. The Department of Revenue states that all parties to the document are liable for the tax, regardless of who agrees to pay it. Florida Department of Revenue, documentary stamp tax.

  2. 02

    Deliver required documents on time

    If the property is a condominium, the buyer is entitled, at the seller’s expense, to a current copy of the declaration, articles, bylaws and rules, the annual financial statement and budget, the FAQ sheet, and, when they apply, the milestone inspection summary and structural integrity reserve study. Fla. Stat. s. 718.503 (2026).

  3. 03

    Allow inspections and keep insurance in place

    Buyers will usually inspect during the period written in the contract. Keep the property insured until closing, and do not cancel a policy early without advice from your insurer and closing agent. See Inspections & Contingencies.

  4. 04

    Sign the deed and review the closing statement

    Before you sign, read every debit and credit: payoff, commissions if any, taxes, title charges, and credits to the buyer. Confirm how you will receive proceeds, and use the same wire-fraud caution as buyers. CFPB, mortgage closing scams.

What this means for you. On a Naples or Marco Island sale, the contract — not “what usually happens” — decides who pays the deed stamps and the owner’s title policy. Read those lines before you accept an offer.

04How this year’s property taxes are usually handled

Collier County real estate taxes are for the calendar year that starts January 1 and is billed later in the year. The Tax Collector’s official pages describe the November-through-March discount calendar (4% in November, then 3%, 2%, and 1%), with the gross amount due in March and delinquency, plus a 3% penalty, on April 1.

When you sell mid-year, the current year’s taxes are usually prorated on the closing statement, so you and the buyer each bear a share of the year. The Tax Collector’s FAQ tells a new owner that the bill is still for the full calendar year and is due in full, because the taxes were probably prorated at closing. If there is a question, the FAQ says to contact the closing agent. The Tax Collector does not redo that math on the bill itself.

Source: Property-tax FAQ. Source: Real estate tax page. Source: Property taxes.

What this means for you. If you sell a Naples homestead in April, expect a tax proration on the closing statement. The bill that arrives later still looks like a full-year bill; the buyer generally pays it and has already been credited or charged for your share at closing.

05Official HOA and condo estoppel fee caps

Before closing, the closing agent usually orders an estoppel certificate from the homeowners’ or condominium association. It states what the seller owes and other official account information. Under s. 718.116, Florida Statutes, a condominium association must issue the certificate within 10 business days after a written or electronic request. If it misses that deadline, it may not charge a fee for that certificate. Similar rules appear in the cooperative and HOA chapters.

The dollar caps in the statute are adjusted for inflation and published by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation. The official DBPR fee sheet — not the unadjusted numbers printed in the statute — is the current cap: not more than $299 for a standard certificate; an additional $119 if it is expedited and delivered within three business days; and up to $179 more if the account is delinquent. Higher aggregate caps apply when one owner requests certificates for many units at once. The next scheduled adjustment is by July 1, 2027.

Source: DBPR, official estoppel certificate fees. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.116 (2026).

What this means for you. If you are selling a condo in downtown Naples or an HOA home in North Naples, ask the closing agent to order the estoppel early, and compare the invoice with the official DBPR cap. A late certificate can mean no fee may be charged.

06After you sell: homestead and portability

Selling a homestead is a change of ownership. The next January 1, the new owner starts over at just value unless an exception in the statute applies. If you buy another Florida homestead, you may be able to transfer part of your Save Our Homes assessment difference (portability), generally if you had a homestead in any of the three immediately preceding years, up to a $500,000 cap under current law.

The official form is Florida Department of Revenue Form DR-501T, Transfer of Homestead Assessment Difference. File it with the new homestead application. In Collier County, new homestead applications must be made in person, generally before March 1. The exemption does not move automatically. If the previous homestead was in another county, the two property appraisers exchange the needed information.

Source: Form DR-501T (PDF). Source: Collier homestead page. Source: Collier Property Appraiser forms. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 193.155 (2026).

What this means for you. If you sell in Pelican Bay and buy another homestead in Collier County, bring Form DR-501T when you file in person. Skipping the form can leave the Save Our Homes benefit behind.

07A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Collect deed, tax bill, association documents, and any flood-claim history.
  • Read who pays which closing costs in the offer before you accept it.
  • Deliver condo or HOA documents the buyer is entitled to, and allow time for the estoppel certificate.
  • Review the closing statement, tax proration, and payoff letter before you sign.
  • If you will buy another Florida homestead, file in person and bring official form DR-501T.

08Questions families often ask

Do I have to make repairs if the buyer’s inspection finds problems?

Only if your signed contract requires it. Many Florida sales use an “as-is” form that still lets the buyer inspect and, during the agreed period, decide whether to go forward. The repair duty, if any, is in the contract you sign, not in a general state repair list. See Inspections & Contingencies.

Will I owe documentary stamp tax when I sell?

A deed transferring Florida real property is subject to documentary stamp tax, generally 70 cents per $100 or fraction of the consideration in Collier County. Who writes the check is set in the contract; the Department of Revenue says all parties remain liable for the tax. Florida DOR.

09Inspections & contingencies

Quick answer
  • The length of an inspection period is the number of days written in your contract. Florida does not set one statewide inspection deadline for every home sale.
  • A four-point inspection and a wind-mitigation form are often requested by insurers. They are not a statewide legal requirement to buy a home.
  • My Safe Florida Home is an official state path to a free wind-mitigation inspection — and, for some homesteaded houses, a grant — after you own and occupy the home as your homestead.

10The inspection period

In a typical Florida residential contract, the parties fill in the number of days the buyer has to inspect the property and, depending on the form, to cancel or to ask for repairs. That number is contractual. Do not assume it is ten days, or any other number, unless that number is written in your agreement.

Buyers in Collier County often hire a general home inspector and, separately, a wood-destroying organism (termite) inspector, plus specialists when the first report needs a closer look — a roof, a sewer camera, a four-point, or a wind-mitigation inspection. This guide does not invent a required list. Ask, in writing, which reports a lender or insurer may also want, and schedule them early enough to finish before the contract deadline.

Source: Florida Realtors. Source: CFPB, owning a home.

What this means for you. If you are buying a 1980s house in Naples or a condo on Marco Island, put the inspection end date on your calendar the day the contract is signed, then book the inspectors immediately. Weekend and holiday closures can eat the window.

11Four-point inspections

A four-point inspection looks at four systems: the roof, the electrical system, the plumbing, and the heating and air-conditioning. Insurers commonly ask for one, especially on an older home, so they can decide whether to write a policy and on what terms. Some residual-market applications also use a four-point form. That is an insurance underwriting request, not a Florida law that says you cannot close without one.

The report is usually prepared by a licensed inspector, contractor, or similar professional the insurer will accept. Ask your insurance agent what form they want, how recent it must be, and whether they need photos of the roof and the panel. Keep that request in writing.

Source: Florida DFS, mitigation notices, inspections, and forms. Source: Florida Office of Insurance Regulation, wind mitigation.

What this means for you. If you fall in love with an older home off Tamiami Trail or in East Naples, ask about a four-point inspection during the inspection period — not the week before closing — so you still have time to shop for insurance if a carrier wants repairs first.

12The official wind-mitigation form

Florida has an official Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form, numbered OIR-B1-1802, published by the Office of Insurance Regulation. Insurers use it to document features that can affect a windstorm premium, such as roof shape, roof covering, and opening protection. The current form took effect April 1, 2026. The official OIR page states that a completed form is valid for up to five years if there are no material changes or inaccuracies.

The Department of Financial Services explains that, since July 1, 2008, admitted carriers must accept a valid form signed by an authorized licensee. The form does not, by itself, guarantee a discount. The insurer still applies its own approved credits to the features the form documents.

Source: Florida OIR, wind-mitigation resources. Source: Florida DFS, mitigation inspections and forms.

What this means for you. If a listing in North Naples already has a wind-mitigation form, ask when it was signed and whether anything has changed — a new roof, new windows, or a new opening-protection system. A form older than five years, or one that no longer matches the house, may not be the form an insurer will accept.

13My Safe Florida Home — after you own the house

My Safe Florida Home is an official state program. It is not part of a purchase contract, and it is not a reason to skip a buyer’s inspection. It is a path you can use after you own the home and it is your homestead.

The official program site says a free inspection is available when the home is a single-family unit on an individual parcel (a detached house, or an attached house not exceeding three stories), it is site-built and owner-occupied, and it qualifies for a homestead exemption under chapter 196, Florida Statutes. The official FAQs say multifamily buildings, condominiums, cooperatives, retirement homes, mobile or manufactured homes, and second, vacation, or rental homes are not eligible for that free inspection.

A separate grant, when funding is available, has additional official rules. The program FAQs state that you need the My Safe Florida Home initial inspection, an insured value of $700,000 or less, and a home built before January 1, 2008, as shown on the Property Appraiser’s site, plus a licensed contractor, a final inspection, and insurer discount information. The official FAQ describes a state match of $2 for every $1 the homeowner spends, up to a $10,000 state contribution, and low-income grants of up to $10,000 with no match. The program is subject to appropriation. The statute says it is not an entitlement. Read the current official pages before you plan on a grant; eligibility and funding can change.

Source: My Safe Florida Home. Source: My Safe Florida Home FAQs. Source: Collier County Property Appraiser.

What this means for you. If you close on a homestead in Golden Gate Estates or suburban Naples, look at My Safe Florida Home after you file for homestead — not as a substitute for the inspections in your purchase contract.

14Licensed wood-destroying organism inspectors

A wood-destroying organism inspection (often called a termite inspection) is a common part of a Florida purchase. Hire someone who is actually licensed. The Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services keeps the official pest-control license search, and it publishes a short consumer page on how to confirm a company is licensed.

Search the company and the individual inspector. Confirm that the license is active and that wood-destroying organism work is within the license. This site does not recommend a particular company.

Source: FDACS pest-control license search. Source: FDACS, how to know if a company is licensed.

What this means for you. Before someone walks a Naples or Marco Island house for termites, take two minutes on the official FDACS search. A license you can look up is more useful than a logo on a truck.

15“As-is” and other contingencies

Florida Realtors and The Florida Bar publish both a standard residential contract and an “As Is” residential contract. On an as-is form, the seller generally sells the property in its existing condition, but the buyer still usually has a negotiated window to inspect and decide whether to proceed. The exact cancelation and repair language is in the form and any addenda you sign. Read that language rather than relying on a summary.

Other common contingencies — financing, appraisal, sale of the buyer’s current home — are also contract terms. If a contingency matters to you, it needs to be in the signed document, with clear deadlines.

Source: Florida Realtors.

What this means for you. “As-is” in a Naples sale usually means you may not be able to demand repairs. It does not usually mean you have to buy the house if the inspection is a problem — if your contract still gives you an inspection period to cancel. Read the filled-in form.

16Lead-based paint and radon

For most housing built before 1978, federal law requires the seller (or landlord) to disclose known lead-based paint and lead-based paint hazards, give the buyer the official pamphlet, and allow a 10-day period to conduct a lead inspection or risk assessment, unless the parties agree otherwise. Older cottages and mid-century homes in Naples can fall in that group. Check the year built on the Collier Property Appraiser parcel record.

Source: U.S. EPA, real estate disclosure.

Florida law also requires a radon gas notice in certain real estate transactions and leases. The statutory notice explains that radon is a naturally occurring radioactive gas, that testing is the only way to know a building’s level, and that the law requires the notice. Read the current text of s. 404.056, Florida Statutes (2026) rather than treating this paragraph as the official notice.

What this means for you. If the parcel record says the house was built in 1972, watch for the federal lead-paint packet and decide, during your inspection window, whether you want a lead test.

17Association and flood reviews

If the home is in an HOA or a condominium, Florida statutes give the buyer a right to disclosures and, in some cases, a short window to cancel after receiving documents. Those deadlines (3 days for a missing HOA disclosure summary; 7 days, excluding weekends and legal holidays, for many condo resales; 15 days for many developer condo sales) are explained in HOA & Condo Rules.

Look up the official Collier flood map and ask about insurance during the same due-diligence window. See Flood Zones & Insurance.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 720.401 (2026). Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.503 (2026). Source: Collier 2024 flood map.

What this means for you. A condo on the Gulf or an HOA in North Naples can take as long to review as the house itself. Request the documents the day the contract is signed, and look up the flood zone in the same week.

18A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Confirm the inspection end date written in your contract, and schedule inspectors immediately.
  • Ask your insurance agent, in writing, whether they want a four-point inspection or the official wind-mitigation form.
  • Look up a termite inspector on the official FDACS license search before you hire.
  • If the home was built before 1978, watch for the federal lead-paint disclosure and pamphlet.
  • Check the Collier flood map, and request HOA or condo documents, before the inspection period ends.

19Questions families often ask

Can I walk away after a bad inspection?

It depends on the contract you signed. Many as-is forms let a buyer cancel during a stated inspection period. Other forms may require a repair request or limit cancelation. The only reliable answer is the language in your agreement and any addenda.

Is a four-point or wind-mitigation inspection required by Florida law to buy a home?

No. Florida law does not make either report a legal requirement to purchase a house. Insurers commonly ask for a four-point inspection, and they use the official Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form when they apply wind credits. Ask your insurance agent and lender what they need, and keep any requirement in writing. Florida OIR wind-mitigation resources.

Can I use My Safe Florida Home before I close?

The official program is built around an owner-occupied, homesteaded, site-built single-family home. Use it after you own the house and file for homestead, not as a substitute for the buyer’s inspections in the contract. My Safe Florida Home FAQs.

20Flood zones & insurance

Quick answer
  • Start with Collier County’s official 2024 flood map (effective February 8, 2024). You can search an address and see whether an elevation certificate is already on file.
  • A standard homeowners policy typically does not cover flood. Flood coverage is a separate policy, often through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer. Citizens does not sell flood insurance.
  • If you have a federally backed mortgage on a home in a Special Flood Hazard Area (zones that start with A or V), flood insurance is generally required. Citizens has its own, additional flood-coverage rules for many of its policies.

21How to look up a Collier County flood zone

Open the county’s official interactive map and type the street address. This is the current Digital Flood Insurance Rate Map (DFIRM) for Collier County, effective February 8, 2024. The same map lets you look for an elevation certificate already on file.

The county floodplain office also points to FEMA’s National Flood Hazard Layer and the FEMA Map Service Center if you want a second official look at the same effective map. FloodSmart.gov is the official consumer site for the National Flood Insurance Program.

Source: Collier 2024 flood map and elevation certificate search. Source: Collier County Floodplain Management. Source: FEMA Map Service Center. Source: FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer. Source: FloodSmart.

On the official map, a Special Flood Hazard Area is a high-risk zone. Those zone names start with A or V (for example, AE or VE). Moderate- or lower-risk areas commonly include Zone X, sometimes shown as shaded or unshaded X. Always read the legend on the exact panel. This is a short orientation, not a substitute for the map.

What this means for you. If you are looking at a house on a canal in Naples, a beach condo on Marco Island, or a home in Golden Gate, look the address up yourself. A listing photo or a neighbor’s memory is not the official zone your lender will use.

22Elevation certificates

An elevation certificate is a form a licensed surveyor prepares to show how a building sits relative to the base flood elevation on the flood map. Insurers and lenders often use it. The Collier County Floodplain Management page tells you to check the county’s Elevation Certificate Map — which is built into the official 2024 flood-map tool — to see whether a certificate is already on file for that property.

If you need a new certificate, the county says it must be completed by a Professional Surveyor and Mapper. The floodplain office can help you understand a certificate that is already on file; it does not replace a surveyor when a new one is required.

Source: Collier flood map (includes elevation certificate search). Source: Collier County Floodplain Management.

What this means for you. Before you pay for a new survey, search the official map. A house in older Naples or along a canal may already have a certificate in the county’s file.

23Preliminary map changes

FEMA and the county have also published preliminary flood maps (a Physical Map Revision, FEMA Project 16-04-1501S, issued March 20, 2025). The county’s floodplain page, as updated in 2026, states that those maps remain preliminary. There had been no Letter of Final Determination as of August 1, 2026, so they are not the effective FIRM you should treat as current for insurance and lending.

The county posted a comparison tool (a Changes Since Last FIRM, or CSLF, viewer) so you can see how a preliminary map would differ from the effective February 8, 2024 map. A 90-day appeal and comment period on the proposed maps began August 19, 2026. The floodplain page does not print a single end date in the same sentence; check that official page to see whether comments are still being accepted, and send comments through the county. The page lists Christopher Mason at the floodplain office as a contact for that process: Christopher.Mason@Collier.gov or (239) 252-2932.

Source: Preliminary map comparison tool. Source: Collier County Floodplain Management.

What this means for you. You can look at a proposed change for a Naples or Marco Island address, but do not plan a purchase or a policy around a preliminary map until FEMA makes it effective. Until then, the February 8, 2024 map is the one that counts.

24Flood insurance, lenders, and Citizens

A homeowners policy and a flood policy are not the same product. FloodSmart explains that flood coverage is generally purchased separately. Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, Florida’s residual-market insurer, says plainly that it does not offer flood insurance and is not authorized to do so. If you need flood coverage, you buy it from the National Flood Insurance Program or a private company that writes flood insurance.

If you have a federally backed mortgage on a building in a Special Flood Hazard Area, flood insurance is generally required. The county floodplain page also notes typical National Flood Insurance Program waiting periods (often 30 days, with stated exceptions) and the Regular Program coverage limits it lists for a residence: $250,000 for the building and $100,000 for contents. Those are program caps, not a quote for any one house.

Citizens has its own rules for many of its Personal Lines residential policies that include wind coverage (condominium unit-owner policies are excepted on Citizens’ official page). Citizens required flood insurance for those policyholders in a designated FEMA flood hazard area on renewals on or after July 1, 2023, regardless of the dwelling’s replacement cost. For all other Personal Lines residential policyholders with wind coverage, flood insurance must be in place to renew on or after:

  • January 1, 2024, if the dwelling replacement cost is $600,000 or more
  • January 1, 2025, if the dwelling replacement cost is $500,000 or more
  • January 1, 2026, if the dwelling replacement cost is $400,000 or more
  • January 1, 2027, for all other Personal Lines residential property insured by Citizens that is required to obtain flood insurance

A later Citizens consumer reminder, posted November 13, 2025, repeats the 2025 and 2026 thresholds. Private companies are not required to use the same flood-eligibility rule. This guide does not invent a typical Naples premium. Ask an insurance agent for a quote on the specific address, and read the current rule on Citizens’ own site.

Source: Citizens, new flood requirements. Source: Citizens, November 2025 flood reminder. Source: Citizens home page. Source: FloodSmart. Source: Collier County Floodplain Management.

What this means for you. If you are shopping for insurance on a $500,000 replacement-cost house in Collier County in 2026, and the quote is a Citizens policy with wind coverage, Citizens’ own site says you need a separate flood policy — even if the house is not in a high-risk flood zone. Confirm that with the agent before you count on the quote.

26Condo buildings and flood disclosures

Florida’s condominium sale statute requires contracts to include a conspicuous statement that homeowners’ insurance policies do not include coverage for damage from flooding, and it requires flood-history disclosures by the developer in the cases the statute covers. Ask for those statements, and for the association’s building and flood insurance information, when you buy a condo in Naples or on Marco Island.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.503 (2026). Source: FloodSmart.

What this means for you. A condo association policy may cover the building, the unit, or neither for flood. Get that in writing during your review period, then decide what you still need to buy.

27A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Look up the address on Collier’s official 2024 flood map and save or print the result.
  • Check whether an elevation certificate is already on file before you order a new one.
  • Ask an insurance agent for homeowners and flood quotes on that address before your inspection period ends.
  • If a quote is from Citizens, read Citizens’ current flood-coverage rules on the official site. Citizens does not sell the flood policy itself.
  • For a condo, ask for the association’s insurance and the flood disclosures required by s. 718.503.

28Questions families often ask

If the house has never flooded, do I still need flood insurance?

Past history is not the same as the official flood zone or a lender’s requirement. FloodSmart recommends considering flood insurance even outside high-risk zones, because floods occur in lower-risk areas too. Your lender may also require it, and Citizens may require it for some of its policies. FloodSmart; Citizens flood requirements.

Can you tell me what flood insurance will cost in Naples?

No. A reliable premium depends on the building, the zone, the coverage, deductibles, and the insurer or NFIP product. This site does not invent average premiums. Get a quote on the specific address, and start with the official Collier flood map so the agent is quoting the right zone.

Is the 2025 preliminary map the one my lender will use?

Not while it remains preliminary. Collier’s floodplain page stated, as of August 1, 2026, that there was no Letter of Final Determination. Until a new map becomes effective, the February 8, 2024 map is the current official map. Collier Floodplain Management.

29Homestead exemption

March 1

Deadline the Collier County Property Appraiser states for new homestead applications for the current year. File in person.

$25,000

The base homestead exemption on assessed value, for all levies including school taxes. Fla. Stat. s. 196.031(1)(a).

$26,411

Maximum additional homestead exemption for 2026, on assessed value above $50,000, for non-school levies. Florida Department of Revenue CPI table, revised January 2026.

Quick answer
  • New homestead applications in Collier County must be made in person, generally before March 1, at the Property Appraiser’s office or a posted seasonal location.
  • For 2026, the first $25,000 of assessed value is exempt from all property-tax levies, and an additional exemption of up to $26,411 applies to assessed value above $50,000 for levies other than school taxes.
  • Homestead also brings the Save Our Homes cap on annual assessment increases, and you may be able to transfer part of that benefit to a new Florida homestead (portability).

30Who qualifies

The Collier County Property Appraiser states that a person who, on January 1, holds legal or equitable title to real property in Florida and who in good faith makes the property their permanent residence — or the permanent residence of a dependent — is eligible for a homestead exemption. You must be a permanent resident of Florida as of January 1 of the year you apply. Florida law allows only one homestead exemption per “family unit.” The Appraiser notes that you are not entitled to the exemption if you or your spouse are receiving a residency-based exemption in another state.

Source: Collier County Property Appraiser, Homestead Exemption. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 196.031 (2026).

What this means for you. If Naples will be your permanent home, plan to be a Florida resident and to own the house on January 1. A second home, or a spouse who still claims a residency exemption in another state, can block the filing.

31What it is worth in 2026

If granted, the exemption provides:

  • A reduction of up to $25,000 from the assessed value for all taxing authorities, including school levies.
  • An additional exemption on the assessed value above $50,000, for all levies except school district levies. The additional amount started at $25,000 and is now adjusted annually for inflation.

The Florida Department of Revenue’s official CPI table, revised January 2026, lists the maximum additional exemption as $26,411 for 2026 (a 2.7% CPI change from the 2025 amount of $25,722). To receive the full additional amount, assessed value must be high enough to use all of it: $50,000 plus $26,411, or $76,411. Below that, the additional exemption is only the portion of assessed value above $50,000.

Source: Florida DOR, additional homestead exemption adjustment. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 196.031(1)(b) (2026).

This is not the same as a flat dollar savings on your tax bill. The dollar savings equal the exempt assessed value multiplied by the millage rates that apply to that slice of value. Millage changes by year and by taxing authority. The Property Appraiser’s parcel search is the official place to see a specific property. Collier County Property Appraiser.

What this means for you. The exemption lowers the assessed value the millage is applied to. It is not a flat “tax cut” of $25,000 or $26,411 off your bill. Look at the parcel record and your tax notice for the actual dollars.

32Senior, veteran, and widow exemptions

Collier County also administers additional exemptions. The amounts below are taken from the Property Appraiser’s official pages. Rules and income limits can change, so read the current page or call the office at (239) 252-8141 before you apply.

  • Additional senior homestead exemption (age 65 and over). The amount specified will not exceed $50,000. For a first application, file Form DR-501 and Form DR-501SC by March 1. You must own and reside on the property as of January 1, already qualify for homestead, be 65 on or before January 1, and meet the household-income limit. For the 2026 exemption, the household’s total adjusted gross income for 2025 must not exceed $38,686. After you are approved, you do not reapply each year while you remain eligible, but you must notify the office in writing by May 1 if your income exceeds the annual limit.
  • Long-term residency senior exemption. Collier County also adopted an additional exemption, equal to the assessed value, for qualifying low-income seniors age 65 or older whose homestead had a just value of less than $250,000 in the first year they apply and who have lived on the same property as their permanent residence for at least 25 years. That reduction applies to the county-tax portion described on the official page.
  • Widow’s and widower’s exemption. A widow or widower who is a permanent Florida resident as of January 1 may qualify for a $5,000 exemption on property they own. Remarriage ends the exemption. It cannot be claimed if the person was divorced before the spouse’s death. Proof of the spouse’s death is required, and you must be a widow or widower as of January 1 of the tax year.
  • Veteran exemptions. A former service member discharged under honorable conditions may qualify for a $5,000 exemption if, as of January 1, they are a permanent Florida resident and have a disability of at least 10% caused by misfortune or incurred during wartime service, supported by a VA letter. A separate exemption is available for an honorably discharged veteran with a service-connected total and permanent disability, on homestead property, with a VA letter. Other official pages describe a combat-related disability discount for certain veterans age 65 or older, a deployed-military exemption, and an exemption for a surviving spouse of a veteran or first responder killed in the line of duty. Read those official pages for the exact tests.

Source: Senior citizen exemption. Source: Widow’s and widower’s exemption. Source: Veterans disability exemptions.

What this means for you. If you are 65 or older and moving into a modest Naples homestead, ask the Property Appraiser about the additional senior exemption and, if you have lived in the same house for 25 years, the long-term residency exemption. Bring income documents. Do not assume you receive them automatically with the basic homestead.

33How to file in Collier County

The Collier County Property Appraiser states that new applications must be made in person before March 1 at the Property Appraiser’s office or at select locations during the regular homestead filing period. If you will not be eligible until the following year, you may pre-file any time after March 1 for the succeeding year. If you move, you must file a new application at the new residence; the exemption does not transfer automatically.

Bring the items the Appraiser lists as required proof of legal residence:

  • Evidence of ownership (deed, tax receipt, or similar).
  • Social Security numbers for all applicants and spouses, as required by s. 196.011(1), F.S.
  • Collier County voter registration, if you vote, dated prior to January 1.
  • A Florida driver license dated prior to January 1.
  • A Florida vehicle registration dated prior to January 1.

Optional, as listed by the Appraiser: a Declaration of Domicile filed with the Clerk of the Circuit Court before January 1 of the year of application. Additional documents may be required (for example, a green card if you are not a U.S. citizen, a complete trust if the property is in a trust, or mobile-home title information). If you or your spouse had a residency-based exemption in another state, the Appraiser requires a document showing that exemption has been removed.

Office hours and seasonal filing locations can change. This page does not list unverified addresses or hours. Use the official homestead page and contact page, or call (239) 252-8141, for current locations and hours. New applications are in person.

Source: Collier homestead page. Source: Property Appraiser contact.

What this means for you. If you close on a Naples home and will live there as your permanent residence, put the in-person filing on your calendar before March 1. Bring the identification the Appraiser lists, not a folder of unofficial printouts.

34If you miss March 1

Missing the March 1 deadline is not always the end of the year. Section 196.011, Florida Statutes, says failure to apply on time generally waives the exemption for that year, with two official paths still open.

  • The value adjustment board shall grant the exemption if you can clearly document that failure to apply by March 1 was the result of a postal error.
  • You may still file an application with the Property Appraiser on or before the 25th day after the appraiser mails the notices required under s. 194.011(1) (the TRIM notice). If you show that you were unable to apply on time, or other extenuating circumstances the appraiser judges sufficient, the appraiser may grant the exemption. If the appraiser does not, you may petition the value adjustment board by that same 25th-day deadline. The statute requires a $15 nonrefundable fee for that petition. The board may grant the exemption for the current year if you are qualified and show particular extenuating circumstances.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 196.011 (2026). Source: Collier homestead page.

What this means for you. If March 1 has passed and you still live in the house as your Collier County homestead, file the late application with the Property Appraiser right away and ask about the TRIM-notice deadline. Do not wait until the next January and assume the year is simply lost — and do not assume a late file is automatically approved.

35The tax bill after you file

The Property Appraiser decides exemptions and value. The Collier County Tax Collector collects the bill. Official Tax Collector pages say taxes are payable in November with a 4% discount, December 3%, January 2%, and February 1%, with the gross amount due in March and delinquency in April (a 3% penalty is added April 1). The bill is for the calendar year starting January 1. If you just bought the home, the FAQ says the bill is for the full year and was probably prorated at closing, so it is due in full.

Source: Collier Tax Collector, property taxes. Source: Property-tax FAQ. Source: Real estate tax page.

What this means for you. Filing for homestead in March changes the taxable value on later bills. It does not cancel the Tax Collector’s discount calendar. Paying in November still saves the most.

36Save Our Homes and portability

Once a property receives the homestead exemption, annual increases in assessed value are capped at the lower of 3% or the CPI change, under s. 193.155, F.S. (often called Save Our Homes). The cap applies to assessed value, not automatically to your tax bill, and it generally resets to just value after a change of ownership, with exceptions listed in the statute.

Portability. If you establish a new homestead and you received a homestead exemption as of January 1 of any of the three immediately preceding years, the new homestead may be assessed at less than just value. In general, you can transfer an assessment difference up to $500,000, with the exact math depending on whether the new home’s just value is higher or lower than the prior home’s. You file a department form with the new homestead application. If the previous homestead was in another county, the two property appraisers exchange the needed information.

The official statute text notes that amendments made by a 2026 act first apply to the 2027 ad valorem tax roll. This page describes the rules as they appear in the 2026 statute used for current filings. For a 2027 move, confirm the current text of s. 193.155, F.S. or ask the Property Appraiser.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 193.155 (2026). Source: Form DR-501T (PDF).

What this means for you. When you move from one Florida homestead to another — for example, from a larger Naples house to a smaller one — bring official form DR-501T when you file the new application in person. The benefit does not travel by itself.

37A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Confirm you will be a Florida permanent resident and own the home on January 1.
  • File in person in Collier County before March 1, with the identification the Appraiser lists.
  • If you are moving from another Florida homestead, file the portability form with the new application.
  • If you miss March 1, ask the Property Appraiser about late-file and Value Adjustment Board options rather than assuming the year is lost.
  • Do not treat an unofficial “tax savings” calculator as official. Use the parcel record and the millage on your notice.

Can I file for homestead online in Collier County?

The Collier County Property Appraiser’s homestead page states that new applications must be made in person. Check that official page for any update before you assume a different process. Collier homestead page.

If I buy in April, can I get the exemption for that year?

Eligibility is tied to January 1. The Appraiser states you must be a permanent resident as of January 1 of the year you apply for the exemption. If you will first qualify the following January 1, the office says you may pre-file after March 1 for the succeeding year. Collier homestead page.

What if I miss the March 1 filing date?

39HOA & condo rules

Quick answer
  • HOA buyers must receive a statutory disclosure summary. If it arrives after you sign, you may cancel within 3 days of receiving it, or before closing, whichever is first.
  • Condo resale buyers are entitled to a list of official documents and generally have 7 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, to cancel after signing and receiving them.
  • After the Surfside collapse, Florida law added milestone inspections and structural integrity reserve studies (SIRS) for many residential condominium and cooperative buildings that are three or more habitable stories.

40HOA disclosure and cancellation

If you are buying a parcel in a community with a mandatory homeowners’ association regulated by chapter 720, the seller must present a disclosure summary before you sign the contract. The summary must cover, among other items, that you will be a member of the association, that recorded covenants will govern use of the property, that you will owe assessments (including possible special assessments), that unpaid assessments can become a lien, and that the statements are only a summary — you should read the governing documents.

If that disclosure summary is not provided before you sign, s. 720.401 says the contract is voidable by written notice within 3 days after you receive the summary, or before closing, whichever occurs first. The right may not be waived, but it ends at closing. This HOA section does not apply to associations already regulated under the condominium, cooperative, timeshare, or mobile home chapters.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 720.401 (2026).

What this means for you. If you are buying in a mandatory HOA in North Naples or on Marco Island, do not sign until you have the statutory disclosure summary, or calendar the 3-day cancelation window if it arrives after you sign.

41Condo documents and cancellation

Developer sales. A contract for a residential condominium unit from a developer generally must state that the buyer may cancel by written notice within 15 days after signing and receiving the required documents. The statute also requires flood-insurance and flood-history disclosures, and, for contracts after December 31, 2024, conspicuous statements about whether a milestone inspection, turnover inspection report, or structural integrity reserve study was completed.

Resales (non-developer). Before the sale, a unit owner who is not the developer must provide, at the seller’s expense, a current copy of: the declaration; articles of incorporation; bylaws and rules; the association’s annual financial statement and annual budget; the inspector-prepared milestone inspection summary, if applicable; the most recent SIRS or a statement that one has not been completed; a turnover inspection report for a turnover inspection performed on or after July 1, 2023, if applicable; and the “Frequently Asked Questions and Answers” document. The buyer is also entitled to a governance form from the division.

Each resale contract must contain a conspicuous clause. In substance, either the buyer acknowledges receiving the core documents more than 7 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, before signing, or the buyer may cancel by written notice within 7 days, excluding those days, after signing and receiving the documents if they were requested in writing. The buyer’s right to void ends at closing. A similar 7-day clause applies, for contracts after December 31, 2024, to the milestone summary, turnover inspection report, and SIRS when those studies apply.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.503 (2026).

What this means for you. On a Naples or Marco Island condo resale, ask for the document package the day the contract is signed and count seven days, skipping weekends and legal holidays, if you still have a cancelation right.

42Milestone inspections (post-Surfside)

Florida’s official condominium information site, run with the Department of Business and Professional Regulation, explains milestone inspections this way. A milestone inspection is a structural inspection of an aging building to determine whether substantial structural deterioration exists, as defined in statute.

Residential condominium and cooperative buildings (including mixed-ownership buildings) that are three or more habitable stories must have a milestone inspection when the building reaches 30 years of age, and every 10 years thereafter — or 25 years, and every 10 years thereafter, if the local enforcement agency has determined that local circumstances require the earlier first inspection. Age is measured from the certificate of occupancy. Single-family, two-family, three-family, or four-family dwellings with three or fewer habitable stories above ground are exempted, according to that official page.

Phase 1 is a visual assessment. Phase 2 is required only if substantial structural deterioration is found. The association must distribute the inspector-prepared summary to unit owners within 45 days of receiving the report, post the summary, and publish the report and summary on the association’s website if the association is required to have one. Repairs indicated by a Phase 2 inspection must generally begin within 365 days after the report is received, unless a local government requires an earlier start.

Source: DBPR condominium inspections. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 553.899 (2026).

What this means for you. If you are looking at a mid-rise condo in the City of Naples or on Marco Island, ask for the milestone inspection summary (or a written statement that one is not required or not done) during your statutory review window.

43Which local office notifies the association

Milestone inspections are a state requirement, but the local building department is the office that works with the association on timing, notices, and permits. Use the office that actually has the building:

  • City of Naples Building Department and the city’s milestone inspection process page, for buildings inside the City of Naples.
  • City of Marco Island permitting information, for buildings inside Marco Island.
  • Collier County Building Permits and Construction and the county’s milestone inspections page, for buildings in unincorporated Collier County.

What this means for you. A condo on Gulf Shore Boulevard in the City of Naples and a building in unincorporated Collier County do not go to the same permitting desk. Start with the city or county page that matches the address.

44Structural integrity reserve studies (SIRS)

A SIRS is a reserve study focused on funding future major repairs and replacement of structural elements. The official DBPR page lists eight categories: roof; structural systems; fireproofing and fire protection systems; plumbing; electrical systems; waterproofing and exterior painting; windows and exterior doors; and other elements over a stated dollar threshold that affect structural integrity (the division is required to update that dollar figure annually based on CPI).

Residential condominium associations with buildings of three or more habitable stories must complete a SIRS at least every 10 years after the condominium’s creation for each building three stories or higher. Official deadlines for existing associations have been set in statute and explained by the division (including a December 31, 2025 completion deadline for many associations, and a rule that a SIRS due alongside a milestone inspection on or before December 31, 2026 may be done at the same time, but not after that date). Read the current official inspections page for the deadline that applies to a specific building, because the legislature has continued to adjust these dates.

As of July 1, 2024, within 45 days after a SIRS is completed, the association must notify unit owners that it is available and submit a report to the division. Starting with budgets adopted on or after January 1, 2025, associations may not waive SIRS reserves and must fund them according to the study, according to the official page.

Unit owners who have concerns can file a complaint with the Division of Condominiums, Timeshares and Mobile Homes, using the official inspections page.

Source: DBPR, milestone inspections and SIRS.

What this means for you. A SIRS is about whether the association is funding the building’s future repairs. Ask for the study, and for any special assessments tied to it, before your cancelation window ends.

45How to check the official SIRS database

The Department of Business and Professional Regulation keeps an official reporting database of condominium and cooperative associations that have submitted a SIRS completion report. You can search it yourself.

  1. 01

    Open the official database

    Go to the DBPR SIRS reporting database. This is a state site, not a private listing service.

  2. 02

    Search the association’s legal name

    Use the legal name of the association, which is often on the declaration or the FAQ sheet, not only the marketing name of the community. The division has published lists for submissions from different periods, so you may need to check more than one list.

  3. 03

    Compare it with what you were given

    A database entry is evidence that a report was submitted. It is not a substitute for reading the study, the budget, or the milestone summary the seller is required to provide. If nothing appears, ask the association whether a study was completed and not yet uploaded, or is not required.

Source: DBPR SIRS reporting database. Source: DBPR inspections page.

What this means for you. If you are buying a condo in Naples or on Marco Island, spend ten minutes in the official database alongside the documents the seller gives you. A missing report is a question to ask, not by itself a reason invented here to walk away.

46Estoppel certificates and official fee caps

Before closing, the closing agent usually orders an estoppel certificate from the association. It states what is owed on the account and other official information. Under s. 718.116, a condominium association must issue it within 10 business days after a written or electronic request. If it misses that deadline, it may not charge a fee for that certificate. Similar timing rules exist for cooperatives and homeowners’ associations.

The current official caps, published by DBPR after the statutory inflation adjustment, are not more than $299 for a standard certificate; an additional $119 if it is expedited and delivered within three business days; and up to $179 more if the account is delinquent. Higher aggregate caps apply to multi-unit requests. The next scheduled update is by July 1, 2027. Use the official DBPR sheet, not the older figures still printed in the statute.

Source: DBPR, official estoppel certificate fees. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.116 (2026).

What this means for you. Ask the closing agent to order the estoppel early on a Naples condo or HOA purchase, and compare the invoice with the official cap.

47What to ask for before you buy

  • The disclosure summary (HOA) or the full condo document package listed in s. 718.503, including the FAQ sheet and budget.
  • The milestone inspection summary or a written statement that one is not required or not completed.
  • The most recent SIRS, or a statement that one has not been completed, and any special assessments or loans tied to reserve funding.
  • Current regular assessments, any pending special assessments, and insurance information, including flood.
  • Rules on rentals, vehicles, guests, and any application the association requires of a buyer.

This is general information, not legal advice. Association documents and the statute control if they conflict with a summary on this page.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.503 (2026). Source: DBPR inspections.

What this means for you. Make a short written list of what you have received and what is still missing. If a Naples association is slow, the statutory cancelation window is the protection — but only if you know when it started and when it ends.

48A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Do not sign until you have the HOA disclosure summary or the condo document package, or calendar the statutory cancelation window if documents come later.
  • Read the budget, the FAQ sheet, and any special-assessment notices.
  • Ask for the milestone inspection summary and the SIRS, or the official statement that they are not required or not done.
  • Confirm what flood and building insurance the association carries, and what you must still buy.
  • If something in the documents is unclear, get advice before the cancelation window ends.

49Questions families often ask

Does every Florida condo need a milestone inspection?

No. The official rule described by DBPR applies to residential condominium and cooperative buildings that are three or more habitable stories, with an exemption for certain one- to four-family dwellings with three or fewer habitable stories above ground. A two-story building is not in that three-story category. DBPR inspections page.

Can an association waive SIRS reserves?

The official DBPR page states that if the budget was adopted on or after January 1, 2025, the association may not waive SIRS reserves and must fund them according to the study. Earlier budgets had a different voting rule that ended with 2025 funding. Confirm the current official page for the year that applies to the association you are considering.

FAQQuestions families often ask

Do I have to make repairs if the buyer’s inspection finds problems?

Only if your signed contract requires it. Many Florida sales use an “as-is” form that still lets the buyer inspect and, during the agreed period, decide whether to go forward. The repair duty, if any, is in the contract you sign, not in a general state repair list. See Inspections & Contingencies.

Will I owe documentary stamp tax when I sell?

A deed transferring Florida real property is subject to documentary stamp tax, generally 70 cents per $100 or fraction of the consideration in Collier County. Who writes the check is set in the contract; the Department of Revenue says all parties remain liable for the tax. Florida DOR.

Can I walk away after a bad inspection?

It depends on the contract you signed. Many as-is forms let a buyer cancel during a stated inspection period. Other forms may require a repair request or limit cancelation. The only reliable answer is the language in your agreement and any addenda.

Is a four-point or wind-mitigation inspection required by Florida law to buy a home?

No. Florida law does not make either report a legal requirement to purchase a house. Insurers commonly ask for a four-point inspection, and they use the official Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form when they apply wind credits. Ask your insurance agent and lender what they need, and keep any requirement in writing. Florida OIR wind-mitigation resources.

Can I use My Safe Florida Home before I close?

The official program is built around an owner-occupied, homesteaded, site-built single-family home. Use it after you own the house and file for homestead, not as a substitute for the buyer’s inspections in the contract. My Safe Florida Home FAQs.

If the house has never flooded, do I still need flood insurance?

Past history is not the same as the official flood zone or a lender’s requirement. FloodSmart recommends considering flood insurance even outside high-risk zones, because floods occur in lower-risk areas too. Your lender may also require it, and Citizens may require it for some of its policies. FloodSmart; Citizens flood requirements.

Can you tell me what flood insurance will cost in Naples?

No. A reliable premium depends on the building, the zone, the coverage, deductibles, and the insurer or NFIP product. This site does not invent average premiums. Get a quote on the specific address, and start with the official Collier flood map so the agent is quoting the right zone.

Is the 2025 preliminary map the one my lender will use?

Not while it remains preliminary. Collier’s floodplain page stated, as of August 1, 2026, that there was no Letter of Final Determination. Until a new map becomes effective, the February 8, 2024 map is the current official map. Collier Floodplain Management.
