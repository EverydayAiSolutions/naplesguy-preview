02Typical costs

Published cost figures are medians from a national survey, not a quote for a specific Naples community. Individual communities set their own fees, and families should ask for a current written rate. Community rates in Naples are generally not published the same way as statewide medians.

Assisted living, Florida median

$5,610 a month, or $67,320 a year, from the CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025, document 282102 (dated 03/02/26), collected July through November 2025.

Home care, Florida median

$32 an hour for a non-medical caregiver, or $6,101 a month at 44 hours a week. That is not a 24-hour quote. More hours, overnight care, or 24-hour care will change the total.

Nursing home, Florida median

$10,342 a month for a semi-private room, and $12,167 a month for a private room, from the same survey.

CareScout also publishes metro-level medians in its official Cost of Care tool, including for the Naples and Cape Coral-Fort Myers areas. The official PDF tables used on this site are statewide and national, so this page does not restate a metro figure that is not in that PDF. See the Naples senior living cost guide for more published figures, including Medicare and Medicaid notes.

03Medicaid help in Florida

Florida’s Medicaid long-term care help for people who need nursing-home-level care in the community, including some assisted living, runs through the Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care program. Eligibility, the waitlist, and enrollment are official state processes. Jeff can help a family understand the steps; he does not decide eligibility.

Florida SMMC Long-Term Care

The Agency for Health Care Administration’s SMMC Long-Term Care page explains how the program works and which state agencies are involved. The official SMMC Long-Term Care FAQs cover screening, the waitlist, enrollment, and services, including assisted living. AHCA also posts a step-by-step page on becoming eligible.

Apply through DCF ACCESS

The Florida Department of Children and Families determines financial eligibility for most Florida Medicaid coverage, including long-term care. Families can apply through the official ACCESS Florida portal. Medical eligibility for Medicaid long-term care is assessed by the Florida CARES program at the Department of Elder Affairs.

SHINE and the Elder Helpline

Florida SHINE (Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders) is the state’s official Medicare counseling program. It is reached through the Florida Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337. The official Helpline page also lists 239-652-6901 for Collier County.

Facilities that list Medicaid

This site keeps a list of facilities that list Medicaid on their official FloridaHealthFinder or Medicare Care Compare profiles. A listing is not an open bed. Confirm payment source and availability directly. More official payment notes are on Costs & Paying and the Medicaid guide.

04Power of attorney and health care surrogate

Legal papers often need to be in place before a parent can no longer sign them. This is general information from official Florida sources, not legal advice. A family should talk with a Florida attorney about the right documents for their situation.

Power of attorney

Chapter 709 of the Florida Statutes is Florida’s power of attorney law, including durable powers of attorney. The Florida Bar’s consumer pamphlet on powers of attorney explains that an agent may sell the principal’s home if the power of attorney was executed with the formalities of a deed and authorizes the sale of the homestead. If the principal is married, the spouse must also join in the sale, as s. 709.2201, Florida Statutes confirms. A power of attorney is only in effect while the principal is alive.

Health care surrogate

Chapter 765 of the Florida Statutes covers health care advance directives, including living wills and the designation of a health care surrogate. A suggested statutory form, revised October 2021 and based on s. 765.203, is posted with FloridaHealthFinder materials. The Florida Bar’s consumer page on advance directives also points to AHCA’s guide and forms. Give copies to the health care provider and to the person named. See also the Legal & Planning guide on this site.

05Probate basics

If a parent has died and the home is in the estate, a court-supervised process may be needed before the house can be sold or the title transferred. A family should work with a Florida attorney. This page does not take the place of that advice.

What probate is

The Florida Bar’s Probate in Florida pamphlet describes probate as a court-supervised process for gathering assets, paying debts, and distributing what remains. It notes that even a simple estate stays open at least through the three-month creditor period. After death, a power of attorney no longer applies; a personal representative appointed by the court handles the estate.

Official Florida rules and the local clerk

Florida’s probate statutes include Chapter 733, Administration of Estates, Chapter 734, and Chapter 735, which includes procedures that may apply to some smaller estates. The Collier Clerk of the Circuit Court probate division is the local court office for probate filings and forms. Whether a particular estate can use a shorter process depends on the facts and the statute; that is a question for a Florida attorney.

FAQQuestions families often ask

What does senior living typically cost in Florida?

The CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025, document 282102 (dated 03/02/26), collected July through November 2025, lists a Florida median of $5,610 a month for assisted living, and $32 an hour for a non-medical caregiver, or $6,101 a month at 44 hours a week. Those are statewide medians, not a Naples community’s price. See the Naples senior living cost guide and the official CareScout Cost of Care tool.

Can Medicaid help pay for assisted living in Florida?

Some people who meet Florida’s rules may get help through the Statewide Medicaid Managed Care Long-Term Care program. That is a state process that can involve a waitlist, a financial application through DCF ACCESS Florida, and a CARES medical assessment. The official SMMC Long-Term Care FAQs explain the steps. For Medicare questions, Florida SHINE counseling is reached through the Elder Helpline at 1-800-963-5337.

Can a power of attorney be used to sell a parent’s home?

Yes, if the power of attorney was executed with the formalities of a deed and authorizes the sale of the homestead. If the parent is married, the spouse must also join in the sale. The Florida Bar power-of-attorney pamphlet and s. 709.2201, F.S. explain this. A power of attorney ends at death. After that, a probate personal representative handles the estate. See the Legal & Planning guide and Chapter 709, Florida Statutes. This is not legal advice.

What is a Florida health care surrogate?

A health care surrogate is someone named to make medical decisions if a person cannot speak for themselves. Chapter 765 of the Florida Statutes covers health care advance directives, including living wills and this designation. A suggested form is posted with FloridaHealthFinder materials. The Florida Bar’s advance-directives page also points to official forms. This is not legal advice.

If a parent has died, how does the house get sold?

If the home is in a probate estate, the personal representative may need court authority to sell it, depending on the will and the type of administration. The Florida Bar’s Probate in Florida pamphlet describes the process and notes that even a simple estate stays open at least through the three-month creditor period. Official rules include Chapters 733, 734, and 735 of the Florida Statutes. The Collier Clerk probate division is the local court office. A Florida attorney should advise on the estate.
