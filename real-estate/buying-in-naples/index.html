01Steps to buying in Florida

Quick answer
  • Get a written pre-approval from a lender before you make an offer, so the price and timing in the contract are realistic.
  • Look up the official Collier flood map and the Property Appraiser parcel record for the address before you are locked in.
  • Florida Housing Finance Corporation publishes official down payment and homebuyer programs. Amounts vary by program and year — use the official site, not a guess.
More about Steps to buying in Florida

Steps to buy a Florida home

  1. 01

    Decide what you can comfortably pay

    Include the monthly loan payment, property taxes, homeowners and flood insurance, and any HOA or condo fee. Property taxes and insurance are not the same every year. The Collier County Property Appraiser’s site is the official place to look up a parcel’s assessed history once you have an address. Collier County Property Appraiser.

  2. 02

    Get pre-approved

    A pre-approval is a lender’s written look at your income, debts, and credit. The CFPB’s owning-a-home tools walk through shopping for a loan and reading a Loan Estimate. CFPB, owning a home.

  3. 03

    Look at homes and neighborhood guides

    This site does not run a live listing search. Neighborhood guides on this site are a starting point for local context. When you tour, look at the flood zone, insurance, and any association rules — not only the list price. Naples neighborhood guides.

  4. 04

    Write and negotiate a contract

    The offer should state the price, closing date, deposits, inspection rights, and who pays which closing costs. See Contract-to-Close Simplified.

  5. 05

    Inspect, review title, and read association papers

    Use the inspection period in your contract. If the property is a condo or in an HOA, use the statutory document and cancellation rights described in HOA & Condo Rules.

  6. 06

    Close, then file for homestead if you qualify

    After you close and the home is your permanent Florida residence as of January 1, you may apply for a homestead exemption in person with the Collier County Property Appraiser, generally by March 1. Homestead Exemption guide.

What this means for you. Treat this as a checklist for a Naples purchase, not a script. The dates that matter are the ones in your contract and, after you move in, the March 1 homestead deadline.

How to look up a Collier County parcel

Open the official Collier County Property Appraiser site and search the street address (or the folio number, if you already have it). The parcel record is the county’s official look at ownership, assessed and taxable value, exemptions, and often the year built. It covers property in the City of Naples and unincorporated Collier County.

Source: Collier County Property Appraiser. Source: Collier 2024 flood map.

What this means for you. Do this on any house you might offer on. In a few minutes you can see whether the seller already has a homestead exemption, what the assessed value is, and — on the flood map — which flood zone the same address sits in.

The official property-tax calendar

The Collier County Tax Collector, not the Property Appraiser, collects the bill. Official pages say property taxes are payable in November with a 4% discount, December 3%, January 2%, and February 1%. The gross amount is due in March. Taxes become delinquent in April. The real-estate tax page adds that the tax is for the calendar year starting January 1, notices are mailed on October 31 of the tax year, and a 3% penalty is added if the tax is delinquent on April 1.

If you close mid-year, the current year’s taxes are usually prorated on the closing statement — then the tax bill that arrives later is still for the full calendar year and is due in full. The Tax Collector’s FAQ tells new owners to contact the closing agent if that is unclear. Questions can go to propertytax@colliertax.com or 239-252-8171. Payments can be mailed to Collier County Tax Collector, 3291 Tamiami Trail East, Naples, FL 34112.

Source: Collier Tax Collector, property taxes. Source: Real estate tax page. Source: Property-tax FAQ.

What this means for you. If you close on a Naples house in January, do not be surprised when a tax bill for the whole year arrives later. The proration at closing was meant to settle up with the seller; you still pay the Tax Collector the full bill, on the discount calendar if you want the savings.

Shopping for a mortgage

Federal consumer rules are designed so you can compare lenders. After you apply, you should receive a Loan Estimate. Before closing, you should receive a Closing Disclosure at least three business days ahead. Read both, and ask about any fee that is unclear.

Source: CFPB Know Before You Owe. Source: Review documents before closing.

HUD also publishes a consumer path for buying a home, including working with a housing counselor if you want a third-party review of your budget. U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development.

What this means for you. Shop more than one loan offer. The Loan Estimate is the official form that lets you compare them side by side, whether you are buying in Naples or unincorporated Collier County.

Help with a down payment

Florida Housing Finance Corporation is the official state housing finance agency. It publishes homebuyer and down payment assistance programs. Program names, income limits, and amounts change. Do not rely on a number you heard secondhand — open the official site and read the current program page, or ask a participating lender. Florida Housing Finance Corporation.

Local city or county assistance, if any, is also program-specific. This guide does not list unofficial program amounts.

Source: Florida Housing Finance Corporation. Source: HUD, buying a home.

What this means for you. If a down-payment program is part of your plan to buy in Collier County, read the current official Florida Housing page or talk to a participating lender. Do not rely on an amount you heard last year.

After you own and homestead the house

My Safe Florida Home is an official state path to a no-fee wind-mitigation inspection and, for some eligible homesteaded houses, a grant to strengthen the home against wind. It is for after you own the home, occupy it, and qualify for a homestead exemption — not a step you complete before closing. Eligibility, grant amounts, and funding can change, and the program is not an entitlement. Details are in Inspections & Contingencies and on the official My Safe Florida Home site.

Source: My Safe Florida Home. Source: My Safe Florida Home FAQs. Source: Homestead Exemption guide.

What this means for you. File for homestead in person with the Collier Property Appraiser, then look at My Safe Florida Home if the house is an eligible site-built single-family home. Do not treat the program as part of your purchase inspection period.

A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Get a written pre-approval and a Loan Estimate you understand.
  • Look up the parcel on the Property Appraiser site and the official Collier flood map.
  • Read the contract’s inspection, association, and closing-cost paragraphs.
  • Compare the Closing Disclosure with your Loan Estimate at least three business days before a mortgage closing.
  • If this will be your permanent home, plan the March 1 homestead filing. Look at My Safe Florida Home only after you own and homestead the house.

02Contract to close

Quick answer
  • Most Florida home sales use a written purchase contract. The Florida Realtors and The Florida Bar publish a widely used form; the signed contract, not custom, is what controls.
  • If you use a mortgage, federal rules require a Loan Estimate after you apply and a Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing.
  • Confirm any wire instructions by phone using a number you already have. Do not trust a last-minute email that changes account numbers.
More about Contract to close

The usual path from contract to close

Once a buyer and seller sign a contract, the clock starts on whatever dates the parties wrote in: the inspection period, the time to apply for a loan, the time to receive association documents, and the closing date. Those dates are filled in on the contract. They are not set by a single Florida statute for every sale.

  1. 01

    The contract is signed

    The contract states the price, the closing date, what personal property stays, which inspections are allowed, and who pays which closing costs. In Florida, the Florida Realtors/Florida Bar contract is commonly used. Ask to read the filled-in form, including the closing-costs paragraph, before you sign. Florida Realtors.

  2. 02

    The buyer applies for a loan, if any

    The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that after you apply for a mortgage, the lender must give you a Loan Estimate. You can compare offers from more than one lender. CFPB, owning a home; Know Before You Owe.

  3. 03

    Inspections, title, and association documents

    During the inspection window in the contract, buyers often hire a home inspector and, where it fits, a wood-destroying organism inspection. The closing or title agent searches title and prepares documents. If the home is in an HOA or condominium, Florida law gives the buyer a right to certain documents and, in some cases, a short window to cancel. Those rules are in the HOA & Condo Rules guide.

  4. 04

    The Closing Disclosure arrives

    For most mortgage loans, you must receive the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before you become legally obligated (consummation). Compare it with your latest Loan Estimate. CFPB, review documents before closing.

  5. 05

    Closing and recording

    Funds are disbursed, documents are signed, and the deed and any mortgage are recorded with the county clerk. In Collier County, the Clerk of the Circuit Court records official records and collects documentary stamp tax and intangible tax when those documents are recorded. Collier Clerk, recording fees.

What this means for you. Think of the signed contract as the calendar for your Naples purchase. Every later step — inspections, loan, association papers, closing — hangs on a date that someone wrote in.

The blanks in a typical Florida contract

Many Naples and Collier County sales use a Florida Realtors / Florida Bar residential contract. This page does not copy that form. What matters for you are the blanks the parties fill in, because those written numbers and checkboxes control the deal.

  • Inspection period. The parties write in the number of days the buyer has to inspect and, depending on the form, to cancel or ask for repairs. There is no single statewide number. See Inspections & Contingencies.
  • Closing date. The day written in the contract, not a default in Florida law.
  • Who pays which costs. The form has lines and checkboxes for who pays owner’s title insurance, documentary stamp tax on the deed, and other closing charges. Florida tax law still holds all parties liable for documentary stamp tax, even if the contract assigns the check to one side. Florida Department of Revenue, documentary stamp tax.
  • Deposits and what stays. The amount of the deposit, and any personal property that conveys, are also filled in on the form you sign.

Source: Florida Realtors. Source: Florida DOR, documentary stamp tax.

What this means for you. Before you sign a Naples contract, sit with the filled-in form and check those four items out loud. If a blank is empty, it is not “whatever is customary” — it is unfinished.

Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure

These two federal forms are meant to make loan costs easier to compare. The Loan Estimate shows the terms and estimated costs shortly after you apply. The Closing Disclosure shows the final figures. The CFPB tells buyers to check the loan amount, interest rate, term, monthly payment, APR, and fees, and to ask questions about any change before they sign.

Source: CFPB Closing Disclosure guide. Source: Know Before You Owe.

Good to know

Cash purchases do not use a Loan Estimate or Closing Disclosure. You should still get a written closing statement from the closing agent that lists every debit and credit.

What this means for you. If you are borrowing to buy in Collier County, put the Closing Disclosure on your calendar: it has to be in your hands at least three business days before you are obligated. Compare it with the Loan Estimate, line by line.

Protect your closing funds

The CFPB warns that criminals impersonate title companies, lawyers, or real estate agents and send last-minute emails that change wire instructions. If you follow a fake instruction, the money can be gone quickly.

  • Do not use wiring instructions that arrive only by email, and do not click a link in an unexpected message to get “updated” account numbers.
  • Confirm payment details by phone with your closing agent, using a phone number you already have from a trusted source — not a number in the suspicious email.
  • If you think you wired money to a criminal, call your bank immediately and ask it to recall the wire, and report the incident to the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov.

Source: CFPB, beware of mortgage closing scams.

What this means for you. If an email the night before a Naples closing says the wiring instructions changed, stop. Call the closing agent on a number from their original engagement letter or website, not from that email.

What happens at closing

At closing, the buyer typically signs loan documents (if any) and closing papers, the seller signs the deed, and the closing agent disburses funds according to the statement. Bring a government-issued photo ID, and ask the closing agent ahead of time how they want to receive funds. Confirm any transfer using the wire-fraud steps above.

Recording in Collier County. After closing, the deed — and any mortgage — are sent to the Collier County Clerk of the Circuit Court to be recorded in the official records. That is how the public record shows the new owner, and how the state taxes on those documents are collected. The Clerk posts recording fees and provides official calculators for a deed and for a mortgage, so you can see documentary stamp tax, intangible tax, and the per-page recording fee before you sit down at the table.

Source: Collier Clerk recording fees. Source: Deed fee calculator. Source: Mortgage fee calculator. Source: Closing Costs in Florida.

What this means for you. A typical Naples closing is not finished in your mind until the Clerk has the deed. Ask the closing agent when they will submit it, and use the Clerk’s calculators if you want to see the tax line before you sign.

A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Read the filled-in purchase contract, including dates and who pays which costs, before you sign.
  • If you are borrowing, compare Loan Estimates and, before closing, compare the Closing Disclosure with the latest estimate.
  • Confirm wire or payment instructions by phone on a number you already have.
  • Ask for association documents early if the home is in an HOA or condominium.
  • Plan for homestead filing after you close and move in, if the home will be your permanent Florida residence.

03Closing costs

$0.70 / $100

Documentary stamp tax on a Collier County deed, per $100 or fraction of consideration. Florida Department of Revenue and s. 201.02, F.S.

$0.35 / $100

Documentary stamp tax on a recorded mortgage, per $100 or fraction of the amount secured. S. 201.08, F.S. There is no cap on the recorded mortgage.

2 mills

Nonrecurring intangible tax: $2 per $1,000 of the obligation secured by Florida real property. S. 199.133, F.S.

Quick answer
  • In Collier County, documentary stamp tax on a deed is 70 cents on each $100, or fraction of $100, of the consideration.
  • A recorded mortgage also has documentary stamp tax of 35 cents per $100 or fraction of the amount secured, plus a one-time intangible tax of 2 mills ($2 per $1,000).
  • Florida title insurance premiums follow a state-set schedule. Who pays owner’s title insurance is negotiated in the contract, not fixed by statute.

04Documentary stamp tax

Florida documentary stamp tax is an excise tax on certain documents, including deeds and recorded mortgages. It is usually paid to the county clerk when the document is recorded.

Deeds. In every Florida county except Miami-Dade, the tax on a deed is 70 cents on each $100, or portion thereof, of the total consideration. Consideration includes money paid or to be paid and the amount of any mortgage or other encumbrance on the property, whether or not it is assumed. If a price is $500,000.01, you still count the extra cent as a full additional $100 unit. The Department of Revenue’s own example: $180,000 of consideration is 1,800 taxable units × $0.70 = $1,260.

Collier County is not Miami-Dade, so the Miami-Dade surtax does not apply here. The Collier Clerk’s recording-fee page lists the same deed rate: $0.70 per $100, rounded up to the nearest hundred.

Source: Florida Department of Revenue, Documentary Stamp Tax. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 201.02 (2026). Source: Collier Clerk recording fees.

Notes and mortgages. On a promissory note signed or delivered in Florida, the tax is 35 cents per $100 or fraction of the obligation, capped at $2,450 on the note itself. On a mortgage, trust deed, or other evidence of indebtedness that is filed or recorded in Florida, the tax is also 35 cents per $100 or fraction of the amount secured, and the Department of Revenue states there is no cap on that recorded mortgage. When both a note and a mortgage exist, the tax is paid on the mortgage at recordation, and a notation is made on the note.

Source: Florida DOR, notes and mortgages. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 201.08 (2026).

What this means for you. On a $500,000 Naples deed, the official deed tax is $3,500, because every $100 or fraction counts as a unit. The Clerk collects it when the deed is recorded.

05Nonrecurring intangible tax

Florida also imposes a one-time nonrecurring tax of 2 mills “on each dollar of the just valuation of all notes, bonds, and other obligations for payment of money which are secured by mortgage, deed of trust, or other lien upon real property situated in this state.” Two mills is $0.002 per dollar, which is $2 per $1,000 of the secured obligation. The Collier Clerk lists the same figure as “$2.00 per $1,000.00” of the actual amount.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 199.133 (2026). Source: Collier Clerk recording fees.

What this means for you. This tax appears only when a loan is recorded against Florida real property. An all-cash Collier County purchase does not include it.

06Title insurance (state-set rates)

Florida title insurance risk-rate premiums are promulgated by rule. A person may not charge a premium other than the premium adopted under the rule, except as the insurance code allows. The original owner’s (or mortgage) schedule in Rule 69O-186.003 is:

Original title insurance rates — Fla. Admin. Code R. 69O-186.003(1)
LiabilityPremium per $1,000
First $100,000$5.75
$100,000 to $1 million, add$5.00
Over $1 million to $5 million, add$2.50
Over $5 million to $10 million, add$2.25
Over $10 million, add$2.00

A fraction of $100 is counted as a full $100. The minimum premium for most conveyances is $100. The owner’s policy is issued for the full insurable value of the premises. A simultaneously issued lender’s policy covering the same land, for an amount not more than the owner’s policy, has a minimum promulgated risk premium of $25; any amount above the owner’s policy is rated at the regular original mortgage rates. Reissue and substitution-loan rates also exist in the same rule when their conditions are met.

The promulgated figure is the risk premium. Title or closing agents may also charge for other services (search, closing, endorsements). Those extra charges are not the same as the state-set premium and can vary. Ask for a written estimate that separates the promulgated premium from other fees.

Source: Rule 69O-186.003, Florida Administrative Code. Source: Text of the rate rule.

What this means for you. Ask the closing agent to show the promulgated title premium as its own line, separate from search or closing fees, so you can check it against the official schedule.

07Collier County recording fees

The Collier County Clerk of the Circuit Court posts recording fees and notes that they are subject to change. As of the Clerk’s page last updated November 11, 2026, the fee to record a letter- or legal-size instrument is $10.00 for the first page and $8.50 for each additional page. The Clerk’s deed and mortgage calculators also state that four party names are included, and that each additional name is $1.00. Plat pages larger than 8½ by 14 inches have a different posted rate ($30 first page, $15 each additional).

The Clerk also provides official deed and mortgage fee calculators. Use those tools, or call the Recording Department, to confirm the current fee before you close.

Source: Collier Clerk recording fees.

What this means for you. Fees can change. Before a Collier closing, run the official deed or mortgage calculator, or call the Recording Department, instead of relying on an old estimate.

08Who pays what in Collier County

Florida law sets the amount of documentary stamp tax and intangible tax. It does not assign those taxes to the buyer or the seller as a matter of custom. The Department of Revenue states that all parties to the document are liable for documentary stamp tax, regardless of which party agrees to pay it.

Who actually writes the check is a contract term. The Florida Realtors/Florida Bar contract, which is widely used, has printed allocations and checkboxes — including options for who selects the closing agent and who pays the owner’s title policy. Local practice often has the seller paying the documentary stamp tax on the deed and the owner’s title policy, and the buyer paying the documentary stamp tax and intangible tax on the mortgage plus the lender’s title policy, but that is practice, not a Collier County ordinance. The only reliable source is the filled-in contract in your transaction. This guide does not invent a “Collier custom” chart.

Source: Florida DOR, liability for the tax. Source: Florida Realtors.

What this means for you. In a Collier County sale, “who usually pays” is not an ordinance. The only reliable source is the filled-in contract in your transaction.

09Official estoppel fee caps

If the home is in a condominium, cooperative, or homeowners’ association, the closing agent usually orders an estoppel certificate. Florida law gives the association 10 business days after a written or electronic request to issue it. If the association misses that deadline, it may not charge a fee for that certificate. See s. 718.116, Florida Statutes (2026).

The current official caps, published by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation after the statutory inflation adjustment, are not more than $299 for a standard certificate; an additional $119 if it is expedited and delivered within three business days; and up to $179 more if the account is delinquent. Higher aggregate caps apply when many units owned by the same person are requested at once: $896, $1,194, $1,791, or $2,985, depending on the number of units. The next scheduled update is by July 1, 2027. Use the official DBPR sheet, not the older dollar figures still printed in the statute.

Source: DBPR, official estoppel certificate fees. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.116 (2026).

What this means for you. On a Naples condo or HOA sale, budget for an estoppel fee up to the official cap, and order it early. This fee is not in the closing-cost estimator below, because it is a cap, not a fixed price.

14Questions families often ask

Is there a cap on documentary stamp tax?

There is a $2,450 cap on the tax on a promissory note or other written obligation under s. 201.08(1)(a). The Department of Revenue states there is no cap on the tax due on a mortgage that is recorded in Florida. There is also no cap stated for the deed tax under s. 201.02 in counties other than as described for Miami-Dade’s separate rate and surtax. Florida DOR.

Why doesn’t the estimator include my lender’s fee?

Lender fees, appraisal fees, survey costs, HOA or condo estoppel fees, prepaid insurance, and commissions are not set by the statutes and rules used in the estimator. They vary by lender, association, and contract. The estimator only runs numbers that have an official, published rate.

16Homestead exemption

March 1

Deadline the Collier County Property Appraiser states for new homestead applications for the current year. File in person.

$25,000

The base homestead exemption on assessed value, for all levies including school taxes. Fla. Stat. s. 196.031(1)(a).

$26,411

Maximum additional homestead exemption for 2026, on assessed value above $50,000, for non-school levies. Florida Department of Revenue CPI table, revised January 2026.

Quick answer
  • New homestead applications in Collier County must be made in person, generally before March 1, at the Property Appraiser’s office or a posted seasonal location.
  • For 2026, the first $25,000 of assessed value is exempt from all property-tax levies, and an additional exemption of up to $26,411 applies to assessed value above $50,000 for levies other than school taxes.
  • Homestead also brings the Save Our Homes cap on annual assessment increases, and you may be able to transfer part of that benefit to a new Florida homestead (portability).
More about Homestead exemption

Who qualifies

The Collier County Property Appraiser states that a person who, on January 1, holds legal or equitable title to real property in Florida and who in good faith makes the property their permanent residence — or the permanent residence of a dependent — is eligible for a homestead exemption. You must be a permanent resident of Florida as of January 1 of the year you apply. Florida law allows only one homestead exemption per “family unit.” The Appraiser notes that you are not entitled to the exemption if you or your spouse are receiving a residency-based exemption in another state.

Source: Collier County Property Appraiser, Homestead Exemption. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 196.031 (2026).

What this means for you. If Naples will be your permanent home, plan to be a Florida resident and to own the house on January 1. A second home, or a spouse who still claims a residency exemption in another state, can block the filing.

What it is worth in 2026

If granted, the exemption provides:

  • A reduction of up to $25,000 from the assessed value for all taxing authorities, including school levies.
  • An additional exemption on the assessed value above $50,000, for all levies except school district levies. The additional amount started at $25,000 and is now adjusted annually for inflation.

The Florida Department of Revenue’s official CPI table, revised January 2026, lists the maximum additional exemption as $26,411 for 2026 (a 2.7% CPI change from the 2025 amount of $25,722). To receive the full additional amount, assessed value must be high enough to use all of it: $50,000 plus $26,411, or $76,411. Below that, the additional exemption is only the portion of assessed value above $50,000.

Source: Florida DOR, additional homestead exemption adjustment. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 196.031(1)(b) (2026).

This is not the same as a flat dollar savings on your tax bill. The dollar savings equal the exempt assessed value multiplied by the millage rates that apply to that slice of value. Millage changes by year and by taxing authority. The Property Appraiser’s parcel search is the official place to see a specific property. Collier County Property Appraiser.

What this means for you. The exemption lowers the assessed value the millage is applied to. It is not a flat “tax cut” of $25,000 or $26,411 off your bill. Look at the parcel record and your tax notice for the actual dollars.

Senior, veteran, and widow exemptions

Collier County also administers additional exemptions. The amounts below are taken from the Property Appraiser’s official pages. Rules and income limits can change, so read the current page or call the office at (239) 252-8141 before you apply.

  • Additional senior homestead exemption (age 65 and over). The amount specified will not exceed $50,000. For a first application, file Form DR-501 and Form DR-501SC by March 1. You must own and reside on the property as of January 1, already qualify for homestead, be 65 on or before January 1, and meet the household-income limit. For the 2026 exemption, the household’s total adjusted gross income for 2025 must not exceed $38,686. After you are approved, you do not reapply each year while you remain eligible, but you must notify the office in writing by May 1 if your income exceeds the annual limit.
  • Long-term residency senior exemption. Collier County also adopted an additional exemption, equal to the assessed value, for qualifying low-income seniors age 65 or older whose homestead had a just value of less than $250,000 in the first year they apply and who have lived on the same property as their permanent residence for at least 25 years. That reduction applies to the county-tax portion described on the official page.
  • Widow’s and widower’s exemption. A widow or widower who is a permanent Florida resident as of January 1 may qualify for a $5,000 exemption on property they own. Remarriage ends the exemption. It cannot be claimed if the person was divorced before the spouse’s death. Proof of the spouse’s death is required, and you must be a widow or widower as of January 1 of the tax year.
  • Veteran exemptions. A former service member discharged under honorable conditions may qualify for a $5,000 exemption if, as of January 1, they are a permanent Florida resident and have a disability of at least 10% caused by misfortune or incurred during wartime service, supported by a VA letter. A separate exemption is available for an honorably discharged veteran with a service-connected total and permanent disability, on homestead property, with a VA letter. Other official pages describe a combat-related disability discount for certain veterans age 65 or older, a deployed-military exemption, and an exemption for a surviving spouse of a veteran or first responder killed in the line of duty. Read those official pages for the exact tests.

Source: Senior citizen exemption. Source: Widow’s and widower’s exemption. Source: Veterans disability exemptions.

What this means for you. If you are 65 or older and moving into a modest Naples homestead, ask the Property Appraiser about the additional senior exemption and, if you have lived in the same house for 25 years, the long-term residency exemption. Bring income documents. Do not assume you receive them automatically with the basic homestead.

How to file in Collier County

The Collier County Property Appraiser states that new applications must be made in person before March 1 at the Property Appraiser’s office or at select locations during the regular homestead filing period. If you will not be eligible until the following year, you may pre-file any time after March 1 for the succeeding year. If you move, you must file a new application at the new residence; the exemption does not transfer automatically.

Bring the items the Appraiser lists as required proof of legal residence:

  • Evidence of ownership (deed, tax receipt, or similar).
  • Social Security numbers for all applicants and spouses, as required by s. 196.011(1), F.S.
  • Collier County voter registration, if you vote, dated prior to January 1.
  • A Florida driver license dated prior to January 1.
  • A Florida vehicle registration dated prior to January 1.

Optional, as listed by the Appraiser: a Declaration of Domicile filed with the Clerk of the Circuit Court before January 1 of the year of application. Additional documents may be required (for example, a green card if you are not a U.S. citizen, a complete trust if the property is in a trust, or mobile-home title information). If you or your spouse had a residency-based exemption in another state, the Appraiser requires a document showing that exemption has been removed.

Office hours and seasonal filing locations can change. Use the official homestead page and contact page, or call (239) 252-8141, for current locations and hours. New applications are in person.

Source: Collier homestead page. Source: Property Appraiser contact.

What this means for you. If you close on a Naples home and will live there as your permanent residence, put the in-person filing on your calendar before March 1. Bring the identification the Appraiser lists, not a folder of unofficial printouts.

If you miss March 1

Missing the March 1 deadline is not always the end of the year. Section 196.011, Florida Statutes, says failure to apply on time generally waives the exemption for that year, with two official paths still open.

  • The value adjustment board shall grant the exemption if you can clearly document that failure to apply by March 1 was the result of a postal error.
  • You may still file an application with the Property Appraiser on or before the 25th day after the appraiser mails the notices required under s. 194.011(1) (the TRIM notice). If you show that you were unable to apply on time, or other extenuating circumstances the appraiser judges sufficient, the appraiser may grant the exemption. If the appraiser does not, you may petition the value adjustment board by that same 25th-day deadline. The statute requires a $15 nonrefundable fee for that petition. The board may grant the exemption for the current year if you are qualified and show particular extenuating circumstances.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 196.011 (2026). Source: Collier homestead page.

What this means for you. If March 1 has passed and you still live in the house as your Collier County homestead, file the late application with the Property Appraiser right away and ask about the TRIM-notice deadline. Do not wait until the next January and assume the year is simply lost — and do not assume a late file is automatically approved.

The tax bill after you file

The Property Appraiser decides exemptions and value. The Collier County Tax Collector collects the bill. Official Tax Collector pages say taxes are payable in November with a 4% discount, December 3%, January 2%, and February 1%, with the gross amount due in March and delinquency in April (a 3% penalty is added April 1). The bill is for the calendar year starting January 1. If you just bought the home, the FAQ says the bill is for the full year and was probably prorated at closing, so it is due in full.

Source: Collier Tax Collector, property taxes. Source: Property-tax FAQ. Source: Real estate tax page.

What this means for you. Filing for homestead in March changes the taxable value on later bills. It does not cancel the Tax Collector’s discount calendar. Paying in November still saves the most.

Save Our Homes and portability

Once a property receives the homestead exemption, annual increases in assessed value are capped at the lower of 3% or the CPI change, under s. 193.155, F.S. (often called Save Our Homes). The cap applies to assessed value, not automatically to your tax bill, and it generally resets to just value after a change of ownership, with exceptions listed in the statute.

Portability. If you establish a new homestead and you received a homestead exemption as of January 1 of any of the three immediately preceding years, the new homestead may be assessed at less than just value. In general, you can transfer an assessment difference up to $500,000, with the exact math depending on whether the new home’s just value is higher or lower than the prior home’s. You file a department form with the new homestead application. If the previous homestead was in another county, the two property appraisers exchange the needed information.

The official statute text notes that amendments made by a 2026 act first apply to the 2027 ad valorem tax roll. This page describes the rules as they appear in the 2026 statute used for current filings. For a 2027 move, confirm the current text of s. 193.155, F.S. or ask the Property Appraiser.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 193.155 (2026). Source: Form DR-501T (PDF).

What this means for you. When you move from one Florida homestead to another — for example, from a larger Naples house to a smaller one — bring official form DR-501T when you file the new application in person. The benefit does not travel by itself.

A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Confirm you will be a Florida permanent resident and own the home on January 1.
  • File in person in Collier County before March 1, with the identification the Appraiser lists.
  • If you are moving from another Florida homestead, file the portability form with the new application.
  • If you miss March 1, ask the Property Appraiser about late-file and Value Adjustment Board options rather than assuming the year is lost.
  • Do not treat an unofficial “tax savings” calculator as official. Use the parcel record and the millage on your notice.

17HOA & condo rules

Quick answer
  • HOA buyers must receive a statutory disclosure summary. If it arrives after you sign, you may cancel within 3 days of receiving it, or before closing, whichever is first.
  • Condo resale buyers are entitled to a list of official documents and generally have 7 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, to cancel after signing and receiving them.
  • After the Surfside collapse, Florida law added milestone inspections and structural integrity reserve studies (SIRS) for many residential condominium and cooperative buildings that are three or more habitable stories.
More about HOA & condo rules

HOA disclosure and cancellation

If you are buying a parcel in a community with a mandatory homeowners’ association regulated by chapter 720, the seller must present a disclosure summary before you sign the contract. The summary must cover, among other items, that you will be a member of the association, that recorded covenants will govern use of the property, that you will owe assessments (including possible special assessments), that unpaid assessments can become a lien, and that the statements are only a summary — you should read the governing documents.

If that disclosure summary is not provided before you sign, s. 720.401 says the contract is voidable by written notice within 3 days after you receive the summary, or before closing, whichever occurs first. The right may not be waived, but it ends at closing. This HOA section does not apply to associations already regulated under the condominium, cooperative, timeshare, or mobile home chapters.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 720.401 (2026).

What this means for you. If you are buying in a mandatory HOA in North Naples, do not sign until you have the statutory disclosure summary, or calendar the 3-day cancelation window if it arrives after you sign.

Condo documents and cancellation

Developer sales. A contract for a residential condominium unit from a developer generally must state that the buyer may cancel by written notice within 15 days after signing and receiving the required documents. The statute also requires flood-insurance and flood-history disclosures, and, for contracts after December 31, 2024, conspicuous statements about whether a milestone inspection, turnover inspection report, or structural integrity reserve study was completed.

Resales (non-developer). Before the sale, a unit owner who is not the developer must provide, at the seller’s expense, a current copy of: the declaration; articles of incorporation; bylaws and rules; the association’s annual financial statement and annual budget; the inspector-prepared milestone inspection summary, if applicable; the most recent SIRS or a statement that one has not been completed; a turnover inspection report for a turnover inspection performed on or after July 1, 2023, if applicable; and the “Frequently Asked Questions and Answers” document. The buyer is also entitled to a governance form from the division.

Each resale contract must contain a conspicuous clause. In substance, either the buyer acknowledges receiving the core documents more than 7 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, before signing, or the buyer may cancel by written notice within 7 days, excluding those days, after signing and receiving the documents if they were requested in writing. The buyer’s right to void ends at closing. A similar 7-day clause applies, for contracts after December 31, 2024, to the milestone summary, turnover inspection report, and SIRS when those studies apply.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.503 (2026).

What this means for you. On a Naples condo resale, ask for the document package the day the contract is signed and count seven days, skipping weekends and legal holidays, if you still have a cancelation right.

Milestone inspections (post-Surfside)

Florida’s official condominium information site, run with the Department of Business and Professional Regulation, explains milestone inspections this way. A milestone inspection is a structural inspection of an aging building to determine whether substantial structural deterioration exists, as defined in statute.

Residential condominium and cooperative buildings (including mixed-ownership buildings) that are three or more habitable stories must have a milestone inspection when the building reaches 30 years of age, and every 10 years thereafter — or 25 years, and every 10 years thereafter, if the local enforcement agency has determined that local circumstances require the earlier first inspection. Age is measured from the certificate of occupancy. Single-family, two-family, three-family, or four-family dwellings with three or fewer habitable stories above ground are exempted, according to that official page.

Phase 1 is a visual assessment. Phase 2 is required only if substantial structural deterioration is found. The association must distribute the inspector-prepared summary to unit owners within 45 days of receiving the report, post the summary, and publish the report and summary on the association’s website if the association is required to have one. Repairs indicated by a Phase 2 inspection must generally begin within 365 days after the report is received, unless a local government requires an earlier start.

Source: DBPR condominium inspections. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 553.899 (2026).

What this means for you. If you are looking at a mid-rise condo in the City of Naples, ask for the milestone inspection summary (or a written statement that one is not required or not done) during your statutory review window.

Which local office notifies the association

Milestone inspections are a state requirement, but the local building department is the office that works with the association on timing, notices, and permits. Use the office that actually has the building:

  • City of Naples Building Department and the city’s milestone inspection process page, for buildings inside the City of Naples.
  • Collier County Building Permits and Construction and the county’s milestone inspections page, for buildings in unincorporated Collier County.

What this means for you. A condo on Gulf Shore Boulevard in the City of Naples and a building in unincorporated Collier County do not go to the same permitting desk. Start with the city or county page that matches the address.

Structural integrity reserve studies (SIRS)

A SIRS is a reserve study focused on funding future major repairs and replacement of structural elements. The official DBPR page lists eight categories: roof; structural systems; fireproofing and fire protection systems; plumbing; electrical systems; waterproofing and exterior painting; windows and exterior doors; and other elements over a stated dollar threshold that affect structural integrity (the division is required to update that dollar figure annually based on CPI).

Residential condominium associations with buildings of three or more habitable stories must complete a SIRS at least every 10 years after the condominium’s creation for each building three stories or higher. Official deadlines for existing associations have been set in statute and explained by the division (including a December 31, 2025 completion deadline for many associations, and a rule that a SIRS due alongside a milestone inspection on or before December 31, 2026 may be done at the same time, but not after that date). Read the current official inspections page for the deadline that applies to a specific building, because the legislature has continued to adjust these dates.

As of July 1, 2024, within 45 days after a SIRS is completed, the association must notify unit owners that it is available and submit a report to the division. Starting with budgets adopted on or after January 1, 2025, associations may not waive SIRS reserves and must fund them according to the study, according to the official page.

Unit owners who have concerns can file a complaint with the Division of Condominiums, Timeshares and Mobile Homes, using the official inspections page.

Source: DBPR, milestone inspections and SIRS.

What this means for you. A SIRS is about whether the association is funding the building’s future repairs. Ask for the study, and for any special assessments tied to it, before your cancelation window ends.

How to check the official SIRS database

The Department of Business and Professional Regulation keeps an official reporting database of condominium and cooperative associations that have submitted a SIRS completion report. You can search it yourself.

  1. 01

    Open the official database

    Go to the DBPR SIRS reporting database. This is a state site, not a private listing service.

  2. 02

    Search the association’s legal name

    Use the legal name of the association, which is often on the declaration or the FAQ sheet, not only the marketing name of the community. The division has published lists for submissions from different periods, so you may need to check more than one list.

  3. 03

    Compare it with what you were given

    A database entry is evidence that a report was submitted. It is not a substitute for reading the study, the budget, or the milestone summary the seller is required to provide. If nothing appears, ask the association whether a study was completed and not yet uploaded, or is not required.

Source: DBPR SIRS reporting database. Source: DBPR inspections page.

What this means for you. If you are buying a condo in Naples, spend ten minutes in the official database alongside the documents the seller gives you. A missing report is a question to ask, not by itself a reason invented here to walk away.

Estoppel certificates and official fee caps

Before closing, the closing agent usually orders an estoppel certificate from the association. It states what is owed on the account and other official information. Under s. 718.116, a condominium association must issue it within 10 business days after a written or electronic request. If it misses that deadline, it may not charge a fee for that certificate. Similar timing rules exist for cooperatives and homeowners’ associations.

The current official caps, published by DBPR after the statutory inflation adjustment, are not more than $299 for a standard certificate; an additional $119 if it is expedited and delivered within three business days; and up to $179 more if the account is delinquent. Higher aggregate caps apply to multi-unit requests. The next scheduled update is by July 1, 2027. Use the official DBPR sheet, not the older figures still printed in the statute.

Source: DBPR, official estoppel certificate fees. Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.116 (2026).

What this means for you. Ask the closing agent to order the estoppel early on a Naples condo or HOA purchase, and compare the invoice with the official cap.

What to ask for before you buy

  • The disclosure summary (HOA) or the full condo document package listed in s. 718.503, including the FAQ sheet and budget.
  • The milestone inspection summary or a written statement that one is not required or not completed.
  • The most recent SIRS, or a statement that one has not been completed, and any special assessments or loans tied to reserve funding.
  • Current regular assessments, any pending special assessments, and insurance information, including flood.
  • Rules on rentals, vehicles, guests, and any application the association requires of a buyer.

This is general information, not legal advice. Association documents and the statute control if they conflict with a summary on this page.

Source: Fla. Stat. s. 718.503 (2026). Source: DBPR inspections.

What this means for you. Make a short written list of what you have received and what is still missing. If a Naples association is slow, the statutory cancelation window is the protection — but only if you know when it started and when it ends.

A short checklist

A short checklist
  • Do not sign until you have the HOA disclosure summary or the condo document package, or calendar the statutory cancelation window if documents come later.
  • Read the budget, the FAQ sheet, and any special-assessment notices.
  • Ask for the milestone inspection summary and the SIRS, or the official statement that they are not required or not done.
  • Confirm what flood and building insurance the association carries, and what you must still buy.
  • If something in the documents is unclear, get advice before the cancelation window ends.

FAQQuestions families often ask

Is a pre-approval the same as a pre-qualification?

They are not the same thing in practice. A pre-qualification is often a rough conversation. A pre-approval is usually based on documents the lender has reviewed. Ask the lender what it verified, and use the CFPB’s Loan Estimate to compare actual offers. CFPB.

Can I buy without a real estate agent?

Yes. You can also hire a licensed agent to represent you. Florida sales of real estate for others generally require a license under chapter 475, Florida Statutes. Jeff is a licensed real estate agent. This page is general information, not legal advice.

How long does a Florida closing take?

There is no single statutory closing period for every sale. The closing date is the date written in the contract. Many contracts also include an inspection period and loan-application deadlines that the parties fill in. If you need a certain date — for example, because a senior living move is scheduled — put it in the contract and talk it through early.

Do I have to use a particular contract form?

Florida law does not require every private sale to use one form. In practice, many agents use the Florida Realtors/Florida Bar residential contract. Whatever form you use, the signed document is what the parties have agreed to. Florida Realtors.

What if an email says the wiring instructions changed?

Treat that as a warning sign. The CFPB says to confirm instructions by calling a number you already have, not a number or link in the email. CFPB wire-fraud warning.

Is there a cap on documentary stamp tax?

There is a $2,450 cap on the tax on a promissory note or other written obligation under s. 201.08(1)(a). The Department of Revenue states there is no cap on the tax due on a mortgage that is recorded in Florida. There is also no cap stated for the deed tax under s. 201.02 in counties other than as described for Miami-Dade’s separate rate and surtax. Florida DOR.

Why doesn’t the estimator include my lender’s fee?

Lender fees, appraisal fees, survey costs, HOA or condo estoppel fees, prepaid insurance, and commissions are not set by the statutes and rules used in the estimator. They vary by lender, association, and contract. The estimator only runs numbers that have an official, published rate.

Can I file for homestead online in Collier County?

The Collier County Property Appraiser’s homestead page states that new applications must be made in person. Check that official page for any update before you assume a different process. Collier homestead page.

If I buy in April, can I get the exemption for that year?

Eligibility is tied to January 1. The Appraiser states you must be a permanent resident as of January 1 of the year you apply for the exemption. If you will first qualify the following January 1, the office says you may pre-file after March 1 for the succeeding year. Collier homestead page.

What if I miss the March 1 filing date?

File a late application with the Property Appraiser. Under s. 196.011, you may still apply on or before the 25th day after the TRIM notice is mailed, and you may petition the value adjustment board if the appraiser does not grant it. A postal error has a separate path. Fla. Stat. s. 196.011 (2026).

Does every Florida condo need a milestone inspection?

No. The official rule described by DBPR applies to residential condominium and cooperative buildings that are three or more habitable stories, with an exemption for certain one- to four-family dwellings with three or fewer habitable stories above ground. A two-story building is not in that three-story category. DBPR inspections page.

Can an association waive SIRS reserves?

The official DBPR page states that if the budget was adopted on or after January 1, 2025, the association may not waive SIRS reserves and must fund them according to the study. Earlier budgets had a different voting rule that ended with 2025 funding. Confirm the current official page for the year that applies to the association you are considering.
