Buying or selling

Buying or selling in Golden Gate

Buying here?

  • Price range. Medians for the three months to October 2026: $451,269 in 34116 (Golden Gate City area), $569,708 in 34120 and $593,946 in 34117 (Estates areas). Sources: Redfin: 34116 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34120 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34117 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • Move fast in the City. Homes in 34116 sold in a median 28 days, the quickest of the areas in this guide, so have financing and inspections lined up. Source: Redfin: 34116 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • Estates homework. Many Estates homes use a private well and septic system. Get a septic inspection and a well water test. Flood risk varies widely: First Street rates 34117 extreme and 34120 minor. Sources: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide; Florida Department of Health: Onsite Sewage (septic) program; Florida Department of Health: Private well testing; Redfin: 34117 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34120 housing market (Oct 2026)

Selling here?

  • Market speed. Median days on market: 28 in 34116, 82 in 34120 and 80 in 34117. City homes sell fast when priced right; Estates homes take longer. Sources: Redfin: 34116 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34120 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34117 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • What buyers here weigh. Lower prices than coastal Naples and, in the Estates, more land and no HOA. Expect Estates buyers to ask about well, septic, flood and wildfire risk. Sources: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide; Redfin: 34120 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • What to prepare. Septic permits and pump-out records, any well test results, and permits for additions or storm repairs. Sources: Florida Department of Health: Onsite Sewage (septic) program; Collier County permit history and permit search (GMCD portal)
  • Flood disclosure. Florida sellers of residential property must give buyers a written flood disclosure at or before signing the contract, covering known flood damage, flood insurance claims and federal flood aid. Source: Florida Statutes 689.302: flood disclosure in sales of residential property

Condo safety & lender eligibility

  • Collier County’s milestone map covers condo buildings three stories or taller in unincorporated Collier. Source: Collier County Milestone Inspections (map and status lookup)
Lender red flags, in plain English
  • Litigation: Fannie Mae will not buy loans in projects where the association is in pending litigation over safety, structural soundness, habitability or functional use (minor cases can be excepted). Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  • Critical repairs: Projects needing critical repairs are ineligible, including unfunded repairs over $10,000 per unit due within 12 months, or an evacuation order. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  • Reserves under 10%: Fannie Mae’s full review needs a budget that puts at least 10% toward replacement reserves (or an acceptable reserve study). FHA needs reserves of at least 10% of 12 months of assessments. Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.2-02: Full review process (reserves, delinquencies); HUD Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (condo project standards) (PDF)
  • Delinquent dues: Fannie Mae and FHA both cap owners behind on dues at 15% of units (60+ days for Fannie Mae). Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.2-02: Full review process (reserves, delinquencies); HUD Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (condo project standards) (PDF)
  • Owner-occupancy: FHA generally wants at least 50% of units owner-occupied in existing projects, and insures no more than 50% of units. Sources: HUD: FHA issues new condominium approval rule (2019); HUD Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (condo project standards) (PDF)
  • Single-entity ownership: Fannie Mae treats a project of 21 or more units as ineligible if one person or company owns more than 20% of the units. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  • Special assessments: Lenders must review each special assessment’s purpose, amount, balance left and payoff date; one tied to an unfixed critical repair makes the project ineligible. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects

Mortgage & lender checklist

  1. Check whether the condo is on HUD’s FHA-approved list.HUD: FHA-approved condominium lookup
  2. Get the association’s answers on reserves, delinquencies, litigation, single-entity ownership and critical repairs.Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.2-02: Full review process (reserves, delinquencies) · Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  3. For buildings three stories or taller: the milestone inspection report and the SIRS.Fla. Stat. 553.899 (milestone inspections) · Fla. Stat. 718.112 (condo bylaws: budgets, reserves, SIRS)
  4. Look up the flood zone and get a flood insurance quote before you lock a rate.FEMA Flood Map Service Center · FloodSmart (National Flood Insurance Program)
  5. Get homeowners and wind insurance quotes early.Citizens Property Insurance Corporation · Florida Office of Insurance Regulation
  6. Give your lender every yearly assessment: HOA, CDD and county assessments from the tax bill.Collier County Tax Collector: Property tax search (your parcel’s millage and assessments)

Buyer due-diligence checklist

  1. Pull the Property Appraiser record: owner, year built, recorded sales.Collier County Property Appraiser
  2. Read the real tax bill, including CDD and other non-ad valorem lines.Collier County Tax Collector: Property tax search (your parcel’s millage and assessments)
  3. Check permit history and open permits.Collier County permit history and permit search (GMCD portal) · City of Naples Building: Citizen Self Serve permit search
  4. Check code enforcement cases.Collier County CityView portal (permits, inspections, code cases)
  5. Search recorded liens, judgments and the declaration.Collier County Clerk: Official Records search (deeds, declarations, plats)
  6. Read the flood disclosure and check the FEMA zone.Florida Statutes 689.302: flood disclosure in sales of residential property · FEMA Flood Map Service Center
  7. Get the HOA disclosure summary and the CDD notice.Fla. Stat. 720.401 (HOA disclosure summary for buyers) · Fla. Stat. 190.048 (CDD disclosure to buyers)
  8. For a condo: the resale disclosures, including milestone and SIRS.Florida Statutes 718.503: condo resale disclosures, incl. milestone and SIRS
  9. Confirm the association is active on Sunbiz.Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz): search by entity name
  10. Confirm zoned schools for the address.Collier County Public Schools: attendance boundaries

Questions to ask (not public online)

  • For a condo or deed-restricted street: rental, pet and approval rules.
  • Septic age, last pump-out and inspection; well water test results.
  • Has the home flooded, and which flood zone is it in?
  • Will your lender use Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac or FHA rules for this property, and does the project pass?
  • Is the community age-restricted (55+)? Check the declaration.

Price

Golden Gate City’s 34116 median ($451,269) is less than half the 34108 median that includes Pelican Bay and about a sixth of Old Naples’. Estates medians run $570,000 to $594,000 with land included.

Fees

The platted city has no master HOA and no CDD, and much of the Estates has none. Golden Gate City properties do pay the Golden Gate Beautification MSTU, a county taxing unit set at 0.5 mills.

Sources: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate City guide; Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide; Golden Gate Beautification MSTU agenda packet, Sept 15, 2026 (PDF)

Flood type

Inland Golden Gate avoids direct Gulf surge but deals with rain and canal flooding. Flood scores swing by ZIP: 34120 rates Minor, 34117 Extreme.

Sources: Redfin: 34120 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34117 housing market (Oct 2026); WINK News: Older homes face flooding concerns in Golden Gate Estates

Utilities

Most Estates homes run on a private well and septic system. In Golden Gate City, DRG reports the county says the northeast quadrant has county water and sewer, while other lots use wells and septic.

Sources: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide; Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate City guide
Flood, storms & insurance

Flood zones, evacuation and insurance

The biggest cost and risk questions for most buyers here. Check each address.

Flood zone

Look up the parcel on Collier County’s 2024 flood map or FEMA’s map center. Lots next to canals and low spots can map very differently from the house next door. Preliminary new Collier maps cover basins including Faka Union and Southern Coastal.

Sources: Collier County 2024 flood map (DFIRM) lookup; FEMA Flood Map Service Center; Collier County Floodplain Management

Drainage

Big canals are run by the Big Cypress Basin; most smaller canals and roadside ditches are county-maintained. Report persistent street flooding to Collier Road Maintenance.

Source: Collier County Floodplain Management

Evacuation

Golden Gate is inland, but check your zone on Know Your Zone and plan routes; the county’s new Estates bridges are meant partly to add evacuation options.

Sources: Florida Division of Emergency Management: Know Your Zone; Collier County: 47th Ave NE & 62nd Ave NE new bridge and corridor improvements (Mar 2026)

Wildfire and wind

First Street rates wildfire “Moderate” for most properties here and wind “Extreme.” Check the Florida Forest Service risk portal and keep brush cleared around the house.

Sources: Redfin: 34120 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34117 housing market (Oct 2026); Florida Forest Service: Wildfire Risk Assessment Portal
From residents and local news

What locals like and complain about

Paraphrased from the linked news stories and community groups. No quotes are made up.

What people like

  • Room and freedom in the Estates: acre lots and, in much of it, no association rules beyond the county’s. Source: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide
  • Lower prices than the coast and fast sales in Golden Gate City (a median of 28 days on market in 34116). Source: Redfin: 34116 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • Parks with sports fields and programs, from Golden Gate Community Park to Big Corkscrew Island Regional Park. Sources: Collier County Parks: Golden Gate Community Park; Collier County Parks: Big Corkscrew Island Regional Park

Common complaints

  • Rainy-season flooding. Estates residents told WINK News that newer homes built higher push water toward older, lower homes, and that filled ditches leave water nowhere to go. Source: WINK News: Older homes face flooding concerns in Golden Gate Estates
  • Traffic. Gulfshore Business reports backups on Immokalee Road driven by rapid growth in the Estates; the county is adding turn lanes and widening Randall Boulevard and Oil Well Road. Source: Gulfshore Business: Traffic flow to improve with new lanes on Immokalee Road
  • Density. A resident-run campaign opposed a 2026 plan to allow 36 apartments on one Estates lot, and the county formed a Rural Golden Gate Estates Restudy Committee in December 2025 to review the area’s growth rules. Sources: Save Golden Gate Estates (resident campaign site); Collier County: Rural Golden Gate Estates Restudy Advisory Committee

A good fit for

  • Buyers who want land, a workshop, or space between neighbors (Estates). Source: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide
  • First-time and value buyers who want a house, not a condo, closer to I-75 (Golden Gate City). Source: Redfin: 34116 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • People who would rather follow county rules than an HOA. Sources: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate City guide; Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide

Probably not for

  • People who want to walk to the beach or downtown. Source: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate City guide
  • Buyers who don’t want to maintain a well, septic system or large yard. Sources: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide; Florida Department of Health: Onsite Sewage (septic) program
  • Anyone who wants gates, a clubhouse, and uniform streetscapes. Source: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide

Background

About Golden Gate

Golden Gate City is the census-designated place northeast of central Naples, a compact grid that is denser than the land around it. Its 16 county plats hold about 5,200 parcels built from 1964 on: some 4,200 houses plus duplexes, small multifamily buildings and condos. Source: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate City guide

Golden Gate Estates is the huge, mostly rural area to the east: tens of thousands of parcels platted as one-acre-plus lots, many with no homeowners association and with private wells and septic systems. New Publix and Aldi stores opened in the Orangetree area in November 2025. Sources: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide; Gulfshore Business: Orangetree area adds two new grocery stores (Nov 2025)

On the old Golden Gate golf course, Collier County has built The Gate, a public golf course with a driving range, alongside plans for workforce and senior and veteran apartments and a veterans nursing home. Sources: Gulfshore Business: The Gate golf course; Naples Press: Affordable senior apartments increase to 148 units

Questions & answers

Questions to ask, and common questions

Questions to ask before you buy in Golden Gate (6)
  • Is the home on county water and sewer, or a well and septic? When were they last serviced and tested?
  • What is the flood zone, and did the lot or street flood in recent rainy seasons?
  • Is the road paved and county-maintained, and how long is the drive to work at rush hour?
  • Are there recorded deed restrictions, even without an HOA?
  • Can I add a pool, guest house or workshop on this lot under county rules?
  • What will homeowners, wind and flood insurance cost?

Common questions

Does Golden Gate have an HOA?

Golden Gate City’s platted grid has no master homeowners association and no CDD, and much of Golden Gate Estates has no HOA. Condos and some subdivisions have their own associations or recorded restrictions.

Sources: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate City guide; Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide
Are Golden Gate Estates homes on well and septic?

Most are. Plan on a well inspection, a water test and a septic inspection before you buy, and confirm utilities for the exact address.

Sources: Domain Realty Group: Golden Gate Estates guide; Florida Department of Health: Private well testing; Florida Department of Health: Onsite Sewage (septic) program
What do homes cost in Golden Gate?

Redfin medians for the three months ending October 2026 were $451,269 in 34116 (Golden Gate City), $569,708 in 34120 and $593,946 in 34117.

Sources: Redfin: 34116 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34120 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34117 housing market (Oct 2026)
Does Golden Gate flood?

It is inland, so it isn’t on the front line for Gulf surge, but rain and canal flooding are real complaints. First Street flood scores range from Minor in 34120 to Extreme in 34117, so check each parcel.

Sources: Redfin: 34120 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34117 housing market (Oct 2026); WINK News: Older homes face flooding concerns in Golden Gate Estates
What is changing in Golden Gate?

On the old Golden Gate golf course, Collier County has built The Gate, a public golf course, alongside planned workforce and senior and veteran apartments. In the Estates, the county is building new bridges and widening roads.

Sources: Gulfshore Business: The Gate golf course; Naples Press: Affordable senior apartments increase to 148 units; Collier County: 47th Ave NE & 62nd Ave NE new bridge and corridor improvements (Mar 2026)
