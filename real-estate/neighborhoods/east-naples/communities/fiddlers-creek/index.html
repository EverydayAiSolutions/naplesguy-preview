Overview

About Fiddler’s Creek

Fiddler’s Creek calls itself a private club community “set conveniently between” Naples and Marco Island, on just under 4,000 acres next to the Everglades. The developer is still selling new coach homes, single-family homes and custom estates, and the community’s neighborhoods each have their own character. Source: Fiddler’s Creek (developer site)

Two community development districts (CDDs), which are local units of government, maintain shared infrastructure and collect assessments on the county tax bill. Because they are public, their budgets, agendas and audits are posted online, which is rare for a private community. Sources: Fiddler’s Creek CDD #1: meetings and documents; Fiddler’s Creek CDD #2: meetings and documents; Fla. Stat. 189.069 (special district websites: budgets, audits, meetings)

Buying or selling

Buying or selling in Fiddler’s Creek

Buying here?

  • Price range. The developer sells new coach homes from $1.5M and single-family homes from $3M. Resales vary by neighborhood; the 34114 ZIP median of $584,700 covers a much wider area. Sources: Fiddler’s Creek (developer site); Redfin: 34114 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • Check the CDD line. Look up the home’s tax bill for its CDD operations and any bond debt service, which differs by neighborhood. Sources: Collier County Tax Collector (tax bills and non-ad valorem assessments); Fiddler’s Creek CDD #1 May 27, 2026 agenda with per-neighborhood assessment tables (PDF); Fiddler’s Creek CDD #2 adopted FY2027 budget (PDF)

Selling here?

  • Competing with new homes. Buyers can compare your home with the developer’s new coach and single-family homes. ZIP 34114 homes took a median 105 days to sell. Sources: Fiddler’s Creek (developer site); Redfin: 34114 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • What to prepare. Your tax bill showing CDD amounts, whether any bond debt remains, and association estoppels. Sources: Collier County Tax Collector (tax bills and non-ad valorem assessments); Fiddler’s Creek CDD #1 May 27, 2026 agenda with per-neighborhood assessment tables (PDF)
  • Flood disclosure. Florida sellers of residential property must give buyers a written flood disclosure at or before signing the contract, covering known flood damage, flood insurance claims and federal flood aid. Source: Florida Statutes 689.302: flood disclosure in sales of residential property

Debt, reserves and payoff

  • CDD #1: most neighborhoods are paid in full. Marsh Cove pays $5,100 a year in debt service, with $20,432 to $22,849 of principal left per unit after the 2026–27 payment; its bonds’ final principal payment is May 1, 2033. Source: Fiddler’s Creek CDD #1 adopted FY2027 budget (PDF)
  • CDD #2: debt service runs $1,293.03 to $5,032.98 per unit a year by neighborhood, with 8 to 18 years remaining; Mussorie, parts of Lagomar and Oyster Harbor lots outside the replat are paid in full. Source: Fiddler’s Creek CDD #2 adopted FY2027 budget (PDF)
  • CDD #1’s general fund is budgeted to end FY2027 with about $2.19 million, down from a projected $3.90 million. Source: Fiddler’s Creek CDD #1 adopted FY2027 budget (PDF)

Condo safety & lender eligibility

  • Condo buildings three stories or taller in unincorporated Collier appear on the county milestone map. Sources: Collier County Milestone Inspections (map and status lookup); Fla. Stat. 553.899 (milestone inspections)
Lender red flags, in plain English
  • Litigation: Fannie Mae will not buy loans in projects where the association is in pending litigation over safety, structural soundness, habitability or functional use (minor cases can be excepted). Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  • Critical repairs: Projects needing critical repairs are ineligible, including unfunded repairs over $10,000 per unit due within 12 months, or an evacuation order. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  • Reserves under 10%: Fannie Mae’s full review needs a budget that puts at least 10% toward replacement reserves (or an acceptable reserve study). FHA needs reserves of at least 10% of 12 months of assessments. Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.2-02: Full review process (reserves, delinquencies); HUD Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (condo project standards) (PDF)
  • Delinquent dues: Fannie Mae and FHA both cap owners behind on dues at 15% of units (60+ days for Fannie Mae). Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.2-02: Full review process (reserves, delinquencies); HUD Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (condo project standards) (PDF)
  • Owner-occupancy: FHA generally wants at least 50% of units owner-occupied in existing projects, and insures no more than 50% of units. Sources: HUD: FHA issues new condominium approval rule (2019); HUD Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (condo project standards) (PDF)
  • Single-entity ownership: Fannie Mae treats a project of 21 or more units as ineligible if one person or company owns more than 20% of the units. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  • Special assessments: Lenders must review each special assessment’s purpose, amount, balance left and payoff date; one tied to an unfixed critical repair makes the project ineligible. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects

Mortgage & lender checklist

  1. Check whether the condo is on HUD’s FHA-approved list.HUD: FHA-approved condominium lookup
  2. Get the association’s answers on reserves, delinquencies, litigation, single-entity ownership and critical repairs.Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.2-02: Full review process (reserves, delinquencies) · Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  3. For buildings three stories or taller: the milestone inspection report and the SIRS.Fla. Stat. 553.899 (milestone inspections) · Fla. Stat. 718.112 (condo bylaws: budgets, reserves, SIRS)
  4. Look up the flood zone and get a flood insurance quote before you lock a rate.FEMA Flood Map Service Center · FloodSmart (National Flood Insurance Program)
  5. Get homeowners and wind insurance quotes early.Citizens Property Insurance Corporation · Florida Office of Insurance Regulation
  6. Give your lender every yearly assessment: HOA, CDD and county assessments from the tax bill.Collier County Tax Collector: Property tax search (your parcel’s millage and assessments)

  6. Read the flood disclosure and check the FEMA zone.Florida Statutes 689.302: flood disclosure in sales of residential property · FEMA Flood Map Service Center
  7. Get the HOA disclosure summary and the CDD notice.Fla. Stat. 720.401 (HOA disclosure summary for buyers) · Fla. Stat. 190.048 (CDD disclosure to buyers)

Questions to ask (not public online)

  • Foundation, sub-association and amenity dues, and any capital contribution at closing.
  • Which CDD, which bond series, the yearly debt service and the payoff amount for this lot.
  • Minimum lease term, how many leases a year, and any waiting period before a new owner may rent.
  • Pet and buyer-approval rules for this neighborhood.
  • Will your lender use Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac or FHA rules for this property, and does the project pass?
  • Is the community age-restricted (55+)? Check the declaration.

FAQ

Fiddler’s Creek questions people ask

What are the CDD fees in Fiddler’s Creek?

For FY2027, CDD #1’s adopted operations assessment is $1,836.29 per unit and CDD #2’s is $1,797.75 per unit, both collected on the Collier County tax bill. Some neighborhoods also pay bond debt service, such as $5,100 a year in Marsh Cove.

Sources: Fiddler’s Creek CDD #1 adopted FY2027 budget (PDF); Fiddler’s Creek CDD #2 adopted FY2027 budget (PDF); Fiddler’s Creek CDD #1 May 27, 2026 agenda with per-neighborhood assessment tables (PDF)
Is Fiddler’s Creek gated?

Yes. The developer describes it as a private gated community.

Source: Fiddler’s Creek (developer site)
Where can I read Fiddler’s Creek budgets?

Both community development districts post budgets, agendas and minutes on their websites, as Florida law requires of special districts.

Sources: Fiddler’s Creek CDD #1: meetings and documents; Fiddler’s Creek CDD #2: meetings and documents; Fla. Stat. 189.069 (special district websites: budgets, audits, meetings)
