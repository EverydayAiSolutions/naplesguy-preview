Buying or selling

Buying or selling in Old Naples

Buying here?

  • Price range. The ZIP 34102 median sale price was $2,586,174 for the three months to October 2026, up 31.8% from a year earlier. Cottages, condos and estates are mixed together, so compare against recent sales on the same street and of the same type. Source: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • Time to negotiate. Homes took a median 129 days to sell, which usually leaves room for inspections, an elevation review and insurance quotes before you commit. Source: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • Flood and rebuild rules. First Street rates 98% of the ZIP at extreme flood risk. Pull the City flood map and elevation certificate. For a teardown, check the City’s elevation rules for new homes and the permit history. Sources: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026); City of Naples Floodplain Information; City of Naples Building: Citizen Self Serve permit search
  • Condos. Check the City’s milestone inspection lists and DBPR’s SIRS filings for the building before you write an offer. Sources: City of Naples: Milestone Inspections by Year; Florida DBPR: SIRS Reporting and Database (condos)

Selling here?

  • Market speed. Median time on market in 34102 was 129 days, with prices up 31.8% year over year. Price from recent sales of the same type and plan for a longer marketing window. Source: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • What buyers here weigh. Buyers are paying for walkability to the beach and Fifth Avenue South. They also face extreme flood and wind ratings, so expect questions about elevation, storm history and insurance cost. Sources: City of Naples: Old Naples neighborhood (Streets & Stormwater); Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • What to prepare. Your elevation certificate, permits for any storm repairs, and roof, window and door details. Sources: City of Naples Floodplain Information; City of Naples Building: Citizen Self Serve permit search
  • Flood disclosure. Florida sellers of residential property must give buyers a written flood disclosure at or before signing the contract, covering known flood damage, flood insurance claims and federal flood aid. Source: Florida Statutes 689.302: flood disclosure in sales of residential property
  • Condo documents. Condo sellers must give buyers the association documents Florida requires, including the milestone inspection summary and the structural integrity reserve study when the building needs them. Get these from the association early. Source: Florida Statutes 718.503: condo resale disclosures, incl. milestone and SIRS

Condo safety & lender eligibility

  • City of Naples posts milestone inspection lists by year, so you can see which condo buildings are due. Source: City of Naples: Milestone Inspections by Year
  • Florida DBPR lists condo associations that filed a structural integrity reserve study (SIRS). Source: Florida DBPR: SIRS Reporting and Database (condos)
Lender red flags, in plain English
  • Litigation: Fannie Mae will not buy loans in projects where the association is in pending litigation over safety, structural soundness, habitability or functional use (minor cases can be excepted). Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  • Critical repairs: Projects needing critical repairs are ineligible, including unfunded repairs over $10,000 per unit due within 12 months, or an evacuation order. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  • Reserves under 10%: Fannie Mae’s full review needs a budget that puts at least 10% toward replacement reserves (or an acceptable reserve study). FHA needs reserves of at least 10% of 12 months of assessments. Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.2-02: Full review process (reserves, delinquencies); HUD Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (condo project standards) (PDF)
  • Delinquent dues: Fannie Mae and FHA both cap owners behind on dues at 15% of units (60+ days for Fannie Mae). Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.2-02: Full review process (reserves, delinquencies); HUD Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (condo project standards) (PDF)
  • Owner-occupancy: FHA generally wants at least 50% of units owner-occupied in existing projects, and insures no more than 50% of units. Sources: HUD: FHA issues new condominium approval rule (2019); HUD Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (condo project standards) (PDF)
  • Single-entity ownership: Fannie Mae treats a project of 21 or more units as ineligible if one person or company owns more than 20% of the units. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects
  • Special assessments: Lenders must review each special assessment’s purpose, amount, balance left and payoff date; one tied to an unfixed critical repair makes the project ineligible. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B4-2.1-03: Ineligible projects

Questions to ask (not public online)

  • Minimum lease term, how many leases a year, and any waiting period before a new owner may rent.
  • Pet limits (number, size, breed) and whether renters may have pets.
  • Does the condo board approve or interview buyers, and how long does it take?
  • Any special assessment approved or under discussion, the amount per unit and the payoff date?
  • Latest milestone inspection and SIRS: findings, cost and funding plan.
  • Will your lender use Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac or FHA rules for this property, and does the project pass?
  • Is the community age-restricted (55+)? Check the declaration.

Price vs. location

The ZIP 34102 median of $2.59 million is about 2.6 times the 34108 median that includes Pelican Bay and nearly 6 times Golden Gate City’s. You pay for the walk to the Gulf, not for amenities.

Sources: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34108 housing market (Oct 2026); Redfin: 34116 housing market (Oct 2026)

Fees

Single-family owners usually pay no mandatory association dues, a real difference from Pelican Bay’s required Foundation and county assessments. Condo owners pay their building’s association, which can include reserves and special assessments.

Sources: Old Naples Association; Pelican Bay Foundation estoppel, FY2027 (PDF); Fla. Stat. 718.111 (condo official records and website rules)

Flood and storm exposure

During Hurricane Ian the National Hurricane Center measured 6 to 9 feet of storm surge above ground in Naples; the Naples Pier gauge recorded 6.18 feet above high tide before it was destroyed. Inland areas like Golden Gate face rain flooding, not Gulf surge.

Sources: National Hurricane Center: Hurricane Ian report (2023); Redfin: 34116 housing market (Oct 2026)

Lot rules

New homes must be built to the City’s flood elevation rules, which can lift the living floor well above the street. The City’s 2024 flood maps are in effect for construction and insurance.

Source: City of Naples Floodplain Information
Flood, storms & insurance

Flood zones, evacuation and insurance

The biggest cost and risk questions for most buyers here. Check each address.

Flood zone

Zones change block by block. Look up the exact address on the City of Naples flood map tool or the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask the seller for an elevation certificate. The City’s 2024 maps are in effect; FEMA issued preliminary new maps for Collier County and Naples on March 20, 2025, which are not yet effective.

Sources: City of Naples Floodplain Information; FEMA Flood Map Service Center; Collier County Floodplain Management

Evacuation zone

Old Naples sits on the Gulf and Naples Bay. Check your zone on Florida’s Know Your Zone map before you buy, and read the City’s hurricane page for how evacuation orders are announced.

Sources: Florida Division of Emergency Management: Know Your Zone; City of Naples Emergency Preparedness

Flood insurance

Homeowners policies don’t cover flood. Flood insurance is required with a federally backed mortgage in zones starting with A or V, and new NFIP policies have a 30-day wait. The City is a Community Rating System Class 5 community, worth up to a 25% discount on standard NFIP policies.

Sources: City of Naples: Flood Insurance, Are You Covered?; Collier County Floodplain Management; FloodSmart (National Flood Insurance Program)

Wind and roof

First Street rates wind risk “Extreme” for 100% of 34102 properties. Get wind and homeowners quotes, including from Citizens, the state-created insurer, before you go under contract.

Sources: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026); Citizens Property Insurance Corporation; Florida Office of Insurance Regulation
From residents and local news

What locals like and complain about

Paraphrased from the linked news stories and community groups. No quotes are made up.

What people like

  • Walking or biking to the beach, restaurants, galleries and shops instead of driving. Sources: City of Naples: Old Naples neighborhood (Streets & Stormwater); City of Naples: Cambier Park
  • Old Naples Association members have organized since 1978 to protect the area’s “charm and character,” and the group still holds monthly coffees, candidate forums and social events. Source: Old Naples Association
  • City services and city beach parking: city residents can get a beach parking permit. Source: City of Naples: Beach parking permits

Common complaints

  • Parking during season and special events. Downtown business owners told Gulf Coast News the worst crunches line up with the Third Street farmers market and Fifth Avenue art and car shows; the City has revisited underground parking, which some residents doubt because of flooding. Source: Gulf Coast News (WBBH): Naples City Council to discuss underground parking (Feb 2025)
  • Street and canal flooding. Neighbors in Aqualane Shores, just south of Old Naples, formed a group in 2025 to push the City on saltwater, king-tide and rainwater flooding on their street. Source: Gulf Coast News (WBBH): Aqualane Shores neighbors push for flood fixes
  • Aircraft noise. Residents at a January 2026 Naples Airport forum raised low flights, noise and safety; the Old Naples Association has posted bulletins on airport authority appointments. Sources: Gulf Coast News (WBBH): Naples Airport “Plane Talk” forum (Jan 2026); Old Naples Association
  • Big redevelopment. The 4.3-acre “The Avenue” project on Fifth Avenue South went forward after a 2024 settlement with the City, without underground parking or big-box stores. Source: Fifth Avenue South BID newsletter (Sept 2024): The Avenue project

A good fit for

  • People who want to walk to the beach and to dinner and use the car less. Source: City of Naples: Old Naples neighborhood (Streets & Stormwater)
  • Buyers with a high budget who value location over lot size or amenities. Source: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026)
  • Seasonal residents who like a lock-and-leave condo near Fifth Avenue. Source: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026)

Probably not for

  • Buyers who want a gated community, golf, or a clubhouse included in their fees. Source: Old Naples Association
  • Anyone who can’t budget for flood and wind insurance on a low-lying coastal home. Sources: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026); City of Naples: Flood Insurance, Are You Covered?
  • People who dislike seasonal crowds, event traffic and tight parking. Source: Gulf Coast News (WBBH): Naples City Council to discuss underground parking (Feb 2025)

Downtown is walkable, but Redfin rates the whole ZIP “car-dependent” at 39 out of 100 because it includes outlying streets.

Source: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026)

Season

Expect heavier traffic and harder parking from winter into spring and during downtown events.

Source: Gulf Coast News (WBBH): Naples City Council to discuss underground parking (Feb 2025)

Airport and hospital

NCH Baker Hospital Downtown is on the north side of downtown. Naples Airport serves private and charter flights; the nearest airline airport is Southwest Florida International in Fort Myers.

Sources: NCH Baker Hospital Downtown; Gulf Coast News (WBBH): Naples Airport “Plane Talk” forum (Jan 2026)

Not public online: ask for these

  • There is no neighborhood-wide HOA budget to pull: Old Naples has no master association.
  • Each condo association’s budget, reserve study, SIRS and milestone report sits with that association. Florida requires associations with 25 or more units to post them online, but in a login area for owners, so buyers have to request them through the seller or listing agent.
  • Insurance claim history for a specific home is not public. Ask the seller for a CLUE report and any flood claims.

Questions to ask the HOA, condo or CDD

  • The current budget, the most recent reserve study, and the SIRS (for buildings three stories or taller).
  • Milestone inspection reports (phase 1 and phase 2, if required) and the repair plan and timeline.
  • Any special assessments approved or under discussion, and the amount per unit.
  • Master insurance policy, deductibles, and the last two years of premium increases.
  • Rental rules, pet rules and the approval process for new owners.
Background

About Old Naples

Old Naples is the original grid of numbered avenues and streets in the City of Naples, between Naples Bay and the Gulf of Mexico. There is no official boundary; the City treats it as one of its mapped neighborhoods for streets and stormwater work. Source: City of Naples: Old Naples neighborhood (Streets & Stormwater)

Daily life centers on Fifth Avenue South and Third Street South, Cambier Park, and the beach at the west end of the avenues. Homes range from original cottages and mid-century houses, many now torn down and rebuilt, to new single-family homes, Gulf-front and bayfront estates, and condos and townhomes near Fifth Avenue. Sources: City of Naples: Cambier Park; City of Naples: Naples Pier

The Naples Pier, the neighborhood’s best-known landmark, is closed for a full rebuild after Hurricane Ian. The City lists a scheduled reopening of July 2027. Sources: City of Naples: Naples Pier; City of Naples: Naples Pier rebuild project

Questions & answers

Questions to ask, and common questions

Questions to ask before you buy in Old Naples (7)
  • What is the flood zone and base flood elevation, and is there an elevation certificate?
  • Did this home or building take water in Ian (2022), Helene or Milton (2024), and what was repaired, with permits?
  • How old are the roof, windows and doors, and are they rated for hurricanes?
  • For a teardown: what can be built under City zoning and flood rules, and is the structure historic?
  • Is the home on a street the City has flagged for stormwater work?
  • What will flood, wind and homeowners insurance cost per year?
  • For a condo: what do the reserve study, SIRS and milestone reports say, and are assessments coming?

Common questions

Does Old Naples have an HOA?

Old Naples has no neighborhood-wide mandatory homeowners association. The Old Naples Association is a voluntary neighborhood group founded in 1978. Condo buildings have their own associations, and some lots carry recorded deed restrictions, so check the title work.

Source: Old Naples Association
What do homes cost in Old Naples?

The median sale price in ZIP 34102 was $2,586,174 for the three months ending October 2026, up 31.8% from a year earlier, with a median of 129 days on market (Redfin).

Source: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026)
Is Old Naples in a flood zone?

Much of it is low-lying. First Street rates 98% of properties in 34102 at risk of severe flooding over 30 years. The official zone depends on the lot; look it up on the City of Naples flood map or FEMA’s Flood Map Service Center.

Sources: Redfin: 34102 housing market (Oct 2026); City of Naples Floodplain Information; FEMA Flood Map Service Center
Is the Naples Pier open?

No. The City of Naples is rebuilding the pier after Hurricane Ian and lists a scheduled reopening of July 2027.

Source: City of Naples: Naples Pier
Where do Old Naples residents park at the beach?

City of Naples residents can get a beach parking permit for city beach lots such as Lowdermilk Park; visitors pay at meters.

Sources: City of Naples: Beach parking permits; City of Naples: Lowdermilk Park
